India, much like the United States, has a vested ideological
interest in maintaining the perception that it is a democracy
free of racism. Consequently, until recently, Indian officials have publicly
disavowed any possibility of racism within national borders ( McDuie-Ra
2015 ). But scholars have long debated the similarities and
differences between Indian caste and American race (e.g., Beteille 1990
, Cox 1945 ). Indian and US-based activists, too, have
been using transnational parallels between race and caste to marshal
support for populations subject to discrimination since at least the
1960s. These parallels have been drawn repeatedly by both sides
to argue for greater legal representation of oppressed race and
caste groups and to highlight human rights violations against them.
