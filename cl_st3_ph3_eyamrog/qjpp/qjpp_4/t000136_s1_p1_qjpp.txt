Objectives: Plasticity in the growth of body segments between populations
has been researched in relation to migration, temporal change and
high-altitude studies. We study the within population variation in body
segments, thus controlling for some of the environmental and genetic
differences that could be at play in between populations studies.
We test a version of the thrifty phenotype hypothesis, where
the growth of head-trunk and hand are prioritized due to
their functional significance over height and leg growth.
