Studies of LRS and of proximate mechanisms are ideally grounded
in an empirical assessment of variability in a specified phenotype.
Without such data there is a tendency in actual practice
to fall back on the everyday concept of normal—that which
is commonly seen and measured—with all its attendant limitations. Identifying
and quantifying the patterns of phenotypic variation within and between
populations is key to pinpointing the causes and consequences of
this variation.
