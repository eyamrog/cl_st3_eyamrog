Some studies report associations between maternal infections or allergies and
elevated milk lactoferrin, yet others report lack thereof, as reviewed
in (Villavicencio et al., 2017 ). Furthermore, existing data are
too limited to discern the influence of maternal iron nutrition.
Being an iron-binding protein, lactoferrin is capable of nourishing infants
with maternal iron (Miller, 2016 ). Although much of iron
transfer occurs during gestation, lactation is an opportunity to bolster
infant iron status particularly in mature milk (Hirai et al.,
1990 ; Miller, 2016 ). Therefore, milk lactoferrin levels might
be proportionate to maternal iron availability.
