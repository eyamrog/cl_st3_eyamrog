As stated in H 0 1, boys had lower LAZ
and HCAZ than girls throughout infancy up to 12 months.
Adjusted analyses showed that in early life <2 months, boys
had −0.11 (95% CI -0.16, −0.05) lower LAZ, but this
difference increased to −0.24 (95% CI −0.36, −0.13) by 4
to 6 months. From 6 months, boys caught up such
that there was no sex difference from 12 months onwards.
HCAZ showed a similar pattern except that boys were not
different from girls at 0 to 2 months. Adjusted analyses
showed that boys' HCAZ reached a maximum difference of −0.18
(95% CI −0.26, −0.09) at 6 to 8 months, but
thereafter they began to catch up such that there was
no difference between 12 and 18 months. WLZ showed an
inverse pattern to that of LAZ, failing to support the
hypothesis that boys have lower WLZ. Boys had higher WLZ
from birth to 6 months with a maximum adjusted difference
of 0.17 (95% CI 0.78, 0.27) at 2 to 4
months. This difference gradually faded so that the boys had
similar WLZ from 10 months onwards.
