At the trial's endpoint, when children ranged in age from
birth to 22 months, a cross-sectional endpoint study captured information
on maternal and child anthropometry and details of the delivery.
At birth or endpoint, data were obtained on place and
type of delivery (including episiotomy and cesarean delivery), offspring sex,
and birth outcomes. Our interest in these delivery outcomes was
focused on childbirth itself, rather than future health issues. Data
were also collected on where the birth occurred (home, health
institution, or on the way to an institution). Obtaining data
on childbirth complications by maternal self-report is not ideal, as
it may not accurately reflect clinical diagnosis. Moreover, clinical criteria
may themselves be problematic, due to an inherent over-reliance on
physical markers of labor. Inadequate attention has been directed to
the fact that labor is also sensitive to the stress
response, and may, therefore, vary according to the forms of
social support available (Rutherford et al., 2019 ).
