The discussion on how best to relate weight and height
in humans in an index from a statistical perspective goes
back to the 19th century and was extensively evaluated thereafter.
One of the proposed solutions (to divide the weight by
the square of the height) was proposed by Adolphe Quetelet
and was supported subsequently by Ancel Keys as the so-called
Body Mass Index, becoming the backbone of health research and
clinical practice in the modern era (Blackburn & Jacobs, 2014
; Eknoyan, 2007 ; Keys et al., 1972 ). Nevertheless,
among the biological co-factors that contribute to obesity, body height
has been understudied, especially in the public health field in
high-income countries. This is despite the fact that recent research
has pointed out the importance of this factor.
