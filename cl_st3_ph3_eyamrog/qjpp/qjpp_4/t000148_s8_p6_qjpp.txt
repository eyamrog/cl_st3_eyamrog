Biomedical and bioanthropological research on menstrual cycling overlap and diverge
in interesting ways that exemplify how normative views influence our
understanding of the human female body. For more than 130
years, physicians and gynecological researchers, almost all in North America
and Western Europe, expended considerable effort on documenting and debating
the normal duration and predictability (“regularity”) of menstrual cycling in
their fellow country women (Vitzthum, 2009 ). For much of
that time, it was the implicit assumption of such work
that the physiology of these women is necessarily that of
the entire species.
