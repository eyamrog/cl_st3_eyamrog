[Dalits] lived with constant troubles which were not of their
own making. Thus, they never enjoyed a protected working-class stature.
They were class outcastes who had the bare minimum for
survival. They seldom had a stable job or a life.
Owing to financial distress, many turned to drugs and crime
as refuge as they seemed to be the only avenues
open for survival. Fights—verbal and physical—violence, prostitution, sexual abuse, alcohol
and drug abuse were common. These were the circumstances I
grew up in. (p. 6)
