Studies examining the regulation of immunity in relation to social
environment have used several different approaches. Studies of chronic stress
or inequality often focus on inflammatory markers, as above, as
these relate to chronic diseases which are often associated with
economic and social inequality. A number of studies (e.g., Dieckmann
et al., 2020 ; Levine et al., 2017 ; Powell
et al., 2013 ) have examined patterns of RNA expression
associated with the “conserved transcriptional response to adversity” (Cole, 2019
), which is characterized by upregulation of pro-inflammatory responses and
downregulation of type I interferon antiviral response genes and antibody
synthesis genes (Table 1 ). Measures related to the reactivation
of latent retroviruses such as Epstein–Barr virus and cytomegalovirus have
also been used as proxy measures for stress related immunosuppression
(Glaser et al., 1991 ; McDade et al., 2000 ).
However, anti-EBV antibody is an indirect measure and findings have
been mixed (see below).
