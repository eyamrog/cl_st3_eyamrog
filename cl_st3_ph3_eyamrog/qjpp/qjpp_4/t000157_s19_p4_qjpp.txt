Soe children were thinnest in skinfold, with the lowest weight
and BMI SDS (Table 2 ). Yet, we failed to
detect any plausible reason for their thinness. Neither did we
detect any clinical signs of malnutrition or chronic disease, nor
did we find any correlation between the degree of thinness
and measures of the family's economic potential such as parental
education. Rural children from Soe with higher educated parents (mean
of education years of father and mother >12 years, n
= 111) are not heavier ( t (198) = 0.462,
p = .6442 , 95% CI[−0.21, 0.36]), do not have
higher BMI_SDS ( t (198) = 0.909, p = .3645
, 95% CI[−0.16, 0.43]), nor higher skinfold values ( t
(198) = 1.018, p = .3101 , 95% CI[−0.27, 0.86])
than children raised by lower educated parents (mean of education
years of father and mother <12 years, n = 89).
