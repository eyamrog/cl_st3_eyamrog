Ninety years ago, Alexander Paterson (1932 , p. 10), penologist
and His Majesty's Commissioner of Prisons for England and Wales
(1922–1946), observed that prisons had “grown into the fabric” of
every country so much that without them governments would jeopardize
their “civilized” reputation. As the scholars discussed here argue, this
fabric, this carceral culture, exceeds physical manifestations of confinement and
guides the logic that organizes social life today.
