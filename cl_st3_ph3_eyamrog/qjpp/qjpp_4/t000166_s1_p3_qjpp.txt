Results: In spite of an overall poor anthropometric condition, our
participants were relatively fit. As expected, older children performed better
in both, fine motor tasks and the shuttle test. The
girls were better in fine motor skills, and an original
speed-quality trade-off in the drawing was found. However, the magnitude
of difference by grade was greater for boys' fine motor
skills than those of girls'. A network analysis revealed three
specific clusters, (1) perceived competencies, (2) fitness, and (3) fine
motor skills.
