This study finds that, when considering sociodemographic factors, birth seasonality
is to a large extend related to maternal employment status.
Employed and student mothers, usually more affected by seasonality of
work and school calendar, show conception dates strongly structured around
holidays (Christmas and summer months), whereas unemployed and economically inactive
mothers show a completely different seasonal pattern with higher conception
rates during the spring. Additionally, these different seasonal birth patterns
may have to do with the need, willing or capacity
to plan births that arise in different groups of women.
Factors such as the occupation needs or value given to
education may also be playing a role in pregnancy decisions.
