Girls with greater weekly meat consumption frequencies had significantly higher
DHEA-S (U = 29.00, P = .007). The median DHEA-S
level in the lower consumption group was 59.49 pg/mL (IQR:
29.99-104.25 pg/mL) compared to 302.90 pg/mL (IQR: 123.33-430.50 pg/mL) for
girls eating meat more frequently (Figure 2 ). Consuming more
dairy was also significantly associated with DHEA-S levels (U =
33.00, P = .014). BMI was neither correlated with meat
( r = 0.018, P = .934) nor dairy consumption
( r = −0.359, P = .078).
