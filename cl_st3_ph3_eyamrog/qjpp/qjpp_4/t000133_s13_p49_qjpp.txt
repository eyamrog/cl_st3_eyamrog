Scientists have identified a growing category of emerging contaminants in
water due to modern anthropogenic processes and increased laboratory detection
capabilities. Emerging contaminants are substances with a perceived impact on
human or environmental health, but without any published health criteria.
These substances include perfluorinated compounds, water disinfection byproducts, gasoline additives,
pharmaceutical metabolites, and nanoparticles (Lei et al., 2015 ), as
well as microplastics (Richardson & Kimura, 2016 ), plasticizers, pesticides,
whitening agents, hormones, x-ray contrast media, artificial sweeteners, and flame
retardants (Pal, He, Jekel, Reinhard, & Gin, 2014 ). The
number of regulated chemical pollutants, for example, is now but
a tiny fraction of all the anthropogenic chemicals present in
the environment (Daughton, 2004 ).
