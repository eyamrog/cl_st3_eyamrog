This article, within the context of the American Journal of
Human Biology toolkit series, highlights methods available to human biologists
for measuring sedentary time and sedentary behavior in free-living settings.
We discuss objective measures of sedentary time, which are best
suited for measuring total sedentary time and patterns of sedentary
time, as well as subjective methods, which are important for
determining the context of sedentary behavior. Throughout our discussion, we
place particular emphasis on the balance between the practicalities of
using each method in the field (eg, participant compliance, costs
and burden to the researchers, language, or cultural considerations) and
the validity of method (ie, how well the method measures
what it purports to measure). In doing so, we hope
to provide a comprehensive resource to those interested in measuring
sedentary behavior.
