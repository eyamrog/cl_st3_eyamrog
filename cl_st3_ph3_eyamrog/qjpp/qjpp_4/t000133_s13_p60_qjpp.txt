Intermittency refers to nonconstant availability of water service; Intermittency can
be particularly problematic for households when water service availability is
unpredictable (Galaitsi et al., 2016 ; Hamer et al., 2018
). Intermittent water supplies in piped or centralized systems affect
chlorination and increase the risk of microbial contamination of drinking
water supplies (Bain, Cronk, Hossain, et al., 2014 ; Kumpel
& Nelson, 2013 ). In addition, intermittency increases the need
for water storage, which, as previously discussed, increases risk of
water recontamination (Elala, Labhasetwar, & Tyrrel, 2011 ; Levy, Nelson,
Hubbard, & Eisenberg, 2008 ). Intermittency can be assessed observationally
(eg, through participant-observation), using key informant interviews (eg, with water
providers), or with survey and interview data. While the existence
of intermittency and its predictability is fairly easy to document,
detailed data on intermittency would require the collection of costly
and potentially less accurate observational, self-report, or recall data.
