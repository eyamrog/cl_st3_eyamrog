Antigen stimulations are usually done with 100 μl of whole
blood per sample, so it is feasible to conduct them
with finger-prick collections. However, the blood must be fresh (ideally
stimulation is done within an hour of collection) and cannot
be frozen. After stimulation, the supernatant is collected and frozen
(which necessitates a freezer or liquid nitrogen tank). The supernatant
is typically transported frozen and assayed for cytokines using ELISA
or multiplex assays. Cytokine levels in stimulated supernatants are usually
much higher than they are in circulating blood samples. In
principle, therefore, assays could be developed which avoid freezing, for
example, by spotting stimulated samples on filter paper, or by
preserving stimulated cells with PAXgene reagent and performing RNA-seq instead
of cytokine measurement (e.g., Meitern et al., 2014 ; Pulido-Salgado
et al., 2018 ).
