Blood biomarkers are measured conventionally in serum or plasma obtained
from venous blood samples (VBS). For large-scale international surveys, however,
taking VBS is often prohibitively expensive and administratively difficult since
ethical and data protection requirements vary greatly across countries. Hence,
such surveys rely increasingly on minimally invasive microsampling methods. The
collection of dried blood spots (DBS) is the most well-known
among them (Brindle et al., 2010 ; Crimmins et al.,
2014 ; McDade, 2014 ; McDade et al., 2007 ;
Weir, 2008 ). In its Wave 6 in 2015, SHARE
collected analyzable DBS samples with up to five blood spots
from each of the about 24 000 respondents in 12
SHARE countries at their place of residence in order to
complement self-reported with objective health data.
