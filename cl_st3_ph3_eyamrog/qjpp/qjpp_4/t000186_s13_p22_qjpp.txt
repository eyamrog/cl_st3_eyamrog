Key among these are the impacts of nutrition and obesity.
Nutrition likely has direct effects, for example the inflammatory and
anti-inflammatory properties of some foods ( Alcock et al. 2012
, Johnson et al. 2021 , Shivappa et al. 2014
), but also has indirect effects through the accumulation of
adipose tissue, which produces inflammatory cytokines ( Yudkin et al.
1999 ). This accumulation is itself influenced by early-life malnutrition
and infection and may be an adaptive response that evolved
to create a reservoir of visceral fat to be mobilized
for fighting infections ( Urlacher et al. 2016 , Wells
& Cortina-Borja 2013 , West-Eberhard 2019 ). Obesity is therefore
influenced by biotic factors such as exposure to parasites and
microbiome regulation ( Tilg & Kaser 2011 ) and also
interacts with other biotic factors to influence disease outcomes. For
example, the severity of COVID-19 may be determined by an
interaction among obesity, past infection exposure, and current coinfection with
helminths and commensals (see the sidebar titled COVID-19 and the
Hygiene and Old Friends Hypotheses; Figure 4 ).
