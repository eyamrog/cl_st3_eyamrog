Aquagenx's Compartment Bag Test (CBT), for example, quantifies bacterial MPN
in a 100 mL water sample. It is a more
recent option for testing E. coli in low-resource field conditions,
and compares favorably to results from membrane filtration on m1
agar, a common laboratory growth medium (Stauber, Miller, Cantrell, &
Kroell, 2014 ). To conduct a CBT, one collects a
water sample in a sterile 100 mL plastic bottle or
Whirl-Pak Thio Bag, adds a packet of bacterial growth medium,
pours the sample in the compartment bag (and clips and
seals it), incubates the bag for 24 to 48 hours,
scores the MPN test results, and finally uses a chlorine
tablet to disinfect the sample (Aquagenx, 2019 ). This technique
has the advantages of not requiring electricity, bulky equipment, controlled
incubation, or highly-trained technicians, but cannot be easily sterilized and
reused like many other methods.
