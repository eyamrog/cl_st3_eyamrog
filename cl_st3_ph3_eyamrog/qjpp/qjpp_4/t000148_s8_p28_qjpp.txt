Intriguingly, the recognition of marked natural (and normal) variation in
cycle length has not been accorded to the evident variation
in cycle hormones. This difference in perspective is likely due,
in part, to the recency of our ability to measure
hormones in biosamples (blood, saliva, and urine) collected frequently over
the course of a cycle (Vitzthum, 2021 ). With continuing
technological advancements comes the acquisition of more data and the
growing recognition that there is much more hormonal variation than
previously appreciated. It must be kept in mind, however, that
although hormonal measurements are more direct biomarkers than cycle length
of the underlying ovarian physiology, these hormonal measurements are nonetheless
still proxies for physiological processes, the full complexity of which
we are only beginning to grasp (Prior, 2020 ).
