Contrary to elements proposed in some early versions of the
hygiene hypothesis, exposure to infectious crowd diseases is unlikely to
improve health overall. Some evidence indicates that latent herpesvirus infections
such as Epstein-Barr virus and cytomegalovirus can reduce the risk
of allergy and atopy. However, these and other viral infections
may increase susceptibility to other infections such as HIV and
have been associated with increases in inflammatory and autoimmune disorders
( Parker et al. 2021 ), including systemic lupus erythematosus
and atherosclerosis ( White et al. 2012 ). In the
long term, infectious diseases, particularly those that are intracellular, such
as viral infections, can cause negative health outcomes through increases
in thymic output, turnover in naïve T cells, and eventual
accumulation of senescent T cells, which are cells that lose
responsiveness after undergoing too many cycles of replication ( Nikolich-Žugich
2008 , Permar et al. 2003 , Sansoni et al.
2008 ). Depletions such as these may have little effect
early in life but cumulatively accelerate immunosenescence and susceptibility to
infection late in life ( Effros 2005 , Pawelec &
Derhovanessian 2011 , Targonski et al. 2007 ).
