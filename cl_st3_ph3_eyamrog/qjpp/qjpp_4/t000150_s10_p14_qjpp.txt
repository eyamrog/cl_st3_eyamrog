A unique challenge in the accelerometer-based measurement of sedentary time
is distinguishing, from an acceleration and postural standpoint, “true” sedentary
time from other behaviors that can resemble sedentary time, such
as sleep and non-wear time. There are a number of
methods available to help identify and remove sleep and non-wear
from the dataset, including manual evaluations, participant diaries, and automated
algorithms (see Edwardson et al., 2017 ). An example of
a manual protocol is to assume that waking hours are
07:00 to 23:59; however, this technique may introduce large measurement
error due to substantial interpersonal variability in sleep and wake
times (Edwardson et al., 2017 ). Participant diaries are useful
for capturing this variability in sleep schedules as participants record
the time they go to bed and wake up each
day (as well as when/whether they remove the device), but
not all participants remember to complete and return their diaries.
Automated algorithms are very useful for identifying periods of sleep
and non-wear because they do not impose assumptions about when
sleep might occur; for example, an algorithm for activPAL data
available in SAS or STATA identifies sleep as a bout
of sitting/lying lasting ≥5 hours or the longest bout of
sitting/lying (≥2 hours) per 24-hour period, with additional rules to
classify adjacent periods as sleep or awake (Winkler et al.,
2016 ). Whenever these methods are used, it is advised
to do visual checks (eg, producing heatmaps) to ensure that
the algorithm's decisions appear sensible and apply manual corrections if
necessary (Edwardson et al., 2017 ). A toolkit by Samson
( 2020 ) regarding the measurement of sleep may be
the best resource for those looking to measure and remove
sleep time from sedentary behavior analyses.
