Shortening of the ends of chromosomes can lead to instability
and the eventual loss of the whole chromosome (Pampalona et
al., 2010 ). Another proven cytogenetic change associated with aging,
is a decrease in the number of sex chromosomes. In
the case of women, it affects one of the X
chromosomes, whereas in the case of men it affects the
Y chromosome (Guttenbach et al., 1995 ; Russell et al.,
2007 ). Numerous studies have linked the numerical reduction of
sex chromosomes to the development of Alzheimer's disease and malignant
tumors (Dumanski et al., 2016 ; Noveski et al., 2016
), but the relationship between socioeconomic background and cytogenetic aging
has not been studied.
