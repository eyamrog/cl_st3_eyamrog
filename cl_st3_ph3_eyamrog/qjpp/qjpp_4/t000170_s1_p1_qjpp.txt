Objectives: The degree of sexual stature difference (SSD), the ratio
of male to female height, is argued to be an
indicator of living standards based on evidence that physical growth
for males is more sensitive to environmental fluctuations. In a
resource-poor environment, the degree of SSD is expected to be
relatively low. The aim of this study is to comparatively
assess SSD in medieval London in the context of repeated
famine events and other environmental stressors before the Black Death
(BD) and the improved living conditions that characterized the post-Black
Death period.
