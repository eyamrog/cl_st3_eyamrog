When worn on the hip or wrist, accelerometers determine sedentary
time based on periods of non-movement without measuring posture. Historically,
sedentary time has been determined when proprietary counts, usually counts
per minute (cpm), were below a validated threshold, usually <100
cpm on the waist (Matthews et al., 2008 ) or
<1853 cpm on the non-dominant wrist (Koster et al., 2016
). More recently, to avoid reliance on the “black box”
nature of proprietary counts that differ between accelerometer manufacturers (Duncan
et al., 2018 ), there is increasing use of raw
gravitational acceleration in units of milli-gravity (m g ) collected
via accelerometry. In these units, such as the GENEA acceleration
sensor (STMicroelectronics, Geneva, Switzerland), sedentary time has been classed based
on acceleration of <376 m g on the wrist or
<96 m g on the waist when the accelerometer measurement
frequency is 100 Hz (Esliger et al., 2011 ).
