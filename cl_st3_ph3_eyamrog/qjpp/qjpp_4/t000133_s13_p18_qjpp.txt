Osmolality is determined by freezing point depression osmometry, calculated as
milliosmoles per kilogram (mOsm/kg) on an osmometer. The normal range
of a euhydrated or well-hydrated state is serum osmolality between
285 and 295 mOsm/kg. Values of 295 mOsm/kg indicate impending
dehydration. Values above 300 mOsm/kg are used as a cutoff
for dehydration. Values below 285 mOsm/kg may reflect overhydration or
hyponatremia, which can be similarly dangerous and should be examined
for serum sodium levels to further examine electrolyte imbalances (Cheuvront
et al., 2010 ). However, the correlation between Sosm and
urinary biomarkers of hydration are not very strong ( r
= ~.2). Without an osmometer in the lab, generally the
cost is ~$25 per sample.
