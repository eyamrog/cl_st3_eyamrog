Although groundbreaking, early studies of ovarian steroid hormones (progesterone, estrogens)
relied on small samples and/or on assaying only a few
blood samples from each participant, in some cases collecting samples
without regard to cycle day or only timed to the
preceding first day of menstrual bleeding. There was at that
time less appreciation for the full extent of cycle variability
and, in any case, serial daily blood sampling for a
full cycle is prohibitively expensive and demanding for researchers and
study participants alike.
