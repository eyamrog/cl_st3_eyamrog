Albeit overlooked in histories of racializing ideas, Airs, Waters and
Places is not only a seminal work in medical ecology
and the geography of disease, read and translated for centuries
in Pagan Antiquity, Latin and Oriental Christendom and the Islamicate,
but also one of the first scientific texts to establish
“the greatest and most marked differences” (AWP, part 12, Intro)
between Europeans and Asians. A whole section of the treatise
aims to “compare Asia and Europe, and to show how
they differ in every respect, and how the nations (ethneon
[ ἐθνέων ]) of the one differ entirely in physique
from those of the other” (ibid.).
