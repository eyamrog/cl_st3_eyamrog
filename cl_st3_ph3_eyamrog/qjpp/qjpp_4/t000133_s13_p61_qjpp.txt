Water treatment is a common water management strategy to support
health outcomes at the household level (Lilje & Mosler, 2017
). Typically, data collection focuses on conventional forms of household
water treatment (eg, boiling, use of filters, chlorination, and solar
UV disinfection) (Hunter, 2009 ). Since water treatment approaches vary
widely across communities, it may be important to explore local
practices (eg, bleach, settling, straining through cheesecloth). Developing an inventory
of all household water treatment strategies for a community may
require formative research using methods like participant-observation, focus groups, or
interviews. For household assessments, appropriate methods include observational or survey
protocols with checklists assessing water treatment usage and frequency. Since
water treatment can be costly (in terms of time and
resources), the costs of water treatment should be included in
assessments of household water insecurity (McLennan, 2000 ; Shrestha, Thapa,
et al., 2018 ; Shrestha, Aihara, et al., 2018 ).
