The similarity in reported median and mean cycle lengths (circa
28–29 days) in our Bolivian and German samples should not
be taken to be a species norm. The Dogon and
several other populations have median/mean cycle lengths of 28–29 days,
however, median/mean cycle length was 31 days or more in
samples from Japan, India and Papua New Guinea (reviewed in
Vitzthum, 2009 ). Furthermore, predictable 28 or 29 day cycle
lengths are not a reliable indicator of nonpathological functioning. Reported
cycle lengths for the Lese are 28–29 days (Bentley et
al., 1990 ), but many were sterile due to endemic
venereal disease (Strassmann, 1997 ). In the Dogon, “regular menses
were a sign of sterility, not fecundity” (Strassmann, 1997 ).
