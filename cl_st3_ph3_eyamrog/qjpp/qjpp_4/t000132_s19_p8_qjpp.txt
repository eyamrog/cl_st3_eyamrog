To explore the effect of MUC2 on copper-mediated electron transfer,
we followed the oxidation of ascorbate in aerobic copper-containing solutions
by monitoring the decrease in absorbance at 265 nm (
Figure 4 A). Although Cu 2+ bound to MUC2 D1
was reduced by ascorbate ( Figure 3 A), the bound
copper did not undergo futile cycling in the transfer of
electrons from ascorbate to oxygen ( Figure 4 B). Mutation
of MUC2 copper ligands compromised the suppression of redox cycling,
and Muc5b D1, which lacks a Cu 1+ binding site,
was less effective than MUC2 D1 ( Figure 4 B).
Longer MUC2 segments prevented the redox cycling of copper as
effectively as the D1 assembly alone ( Figure 4 C).
These results indicate that MUC2 D1 stabilizes Cu 1+ in
the presence of oxygen in a manner that prevents accumulation
of ROS.
