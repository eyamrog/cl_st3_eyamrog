Table 2 details the descriptive statistics and calculated D indices
from tibial lengths for each time period. With the exception
of the late pre-Black Death estimated female tibial lengths (Shapiro
test: p = .013) and the post-Black Death estimated female
tibial lengths (Shapiro test: p = .019), all datasets for
time period and estimated sex are normally distributed. Overall, tibial
lengths decreased for both estimated males and females from the
early to the late pre-BD time periods. Estimated male tibial
lengths subsequently increased in the post-Black Death time period, though
for estimated females, average tibial length remained much the same.
One way ANOVA analyses indicate that changes in tibial lengths
for estimated males across the time periods is significant (
p = .0542) but not for estimated females ( p
= .476).
