A set of LMERMs were built for each dependent variable
for each vowel. The simplest model in each set excluded
all social factors and was structured as follows: dependent.variable ∼
coda + (1| speaker ) + (1| word ). Subsequent
models added to this the social factors of gender ,
ethnicity , and age ; first individually (three models), then
in combination as pairs (three models) or with all three
factors together (one model), and finally cycling through the various
additive and/or interactive combinations (e.g., gender * ethnicity + age
) between them (seven models), amounting to fifteen models in
total. The most parsimonious model was then determined through pairwise
ANOVA comparisons. The factor intercept levels across all models were
set at: vowel = /e/, coda = other, gender =
man, ethnicity = Mennonite, and age = older. The social
factor intercept levels were all selected to represent groups which
could a priori be expected to have more conservative productions.
Women are well known to be generally more linguistically innovative
than men. Mennonite speakers as a group reside in more
conservative rural communities, in contrast with the other two ethnicities
in the study who reside in the urban center of
Winnipeg. And the speech of younger speakers generally presents a
greater degree of innovation than that of older speakers. Under
this view, significant differences from the intercept levels by any
social factor group may therefore identify relatively innovative productions, given
that they uniformly depart from those of more conservative groups.
The LMERM results are accompanied by data visualizations as two-dimensional
mappings of vowel token density estimate distributions in F1xF2 (z)
space.
