This study has documented the productions of /æ/, /ɛ/, and
/e/ in Manitoba and examined their potential for changes in
production and/or merger in prevelar contexts. It has also revealed
some of the important social factors at play, such as
conservatism and the contrasting effect of adherence to extra-local speech
patterns, in either the maintenance or development of ethnolectal difference
in the province. This work contributes to research concerned with
western Canadian/Prairie/Manitoba English, to the literature on prevelar raising and
merger, and to the study of ethnolectal differentiation in Canada.
All three of these fields represent ever-growing bodies of research,
to which this study offers some important findings to be
integrated into our broader understanding of North American English dialect
patterns.
