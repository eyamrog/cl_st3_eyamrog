Using Hyland’s (2018 ) meta-discourse model, Prommas (2020) found that
Thai writers used significantly less evaluative meta-discourse, including attitudinal lexis
( important ) and hedges ( possible ), than writers
of New Zealand English. They made significantly less use of
features that make interpersonal connections with readers, such as self-mention
( I and we ) and engagement features (imperatives). Thai
writers also provided less elaboration by use of code glosses
(including such as ) and fewer citations.
