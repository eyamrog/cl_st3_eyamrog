Corpus linguistics has firmly established itself as a major area
of research within linguistics. Arguably, one of the most practical
applications of corpora and corpus linguistics has been in the
area of second language (L2) acquisition research. Emerging from the
integration of the fields of corpus linguistics and second language
acquisition, learner corpus research has greatly enhanced our understanding of
how language learners acquire and use their L2 ( Granger,
2002 ). Since its inception in the 1980s ( Granger
et al ., 2015 ), learner corpus research has increasingly
attracted scholarly attention from around the world. This paper highlights
the state of learner corpus research in New Zealand, focussing,
in particular, on lexical and syntactic aspects of learner language.
In doing so, it reviews the learner corpus studies carried
out to date by New Zealand-based researchers, describing the results
and implications of such research in the context of L2
education, and discussing the current status and future prospects of
learner corpus research in New Zealand.
