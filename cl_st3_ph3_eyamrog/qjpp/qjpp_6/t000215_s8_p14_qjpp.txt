Subvertical grooves have been observed primarily in Neanderthals ( Giacobini
et al., 1984 ; Antunes and Santinho Cunha, 1992 ;
Villa and Giacobini, 1995 ; Poisson et al., 2002 ;
Egocheaga et al., 2004 ; Rosas et al., 2006 ;
Estalrrich et al., 2011 ). This trait has also been
described in other fossil and extant hominins, such as Homo
habilis ( Puech and Cianfarani, 1988 ), early Homo (
Brink et al., 2012 ), Middle Pleistocene hominins ( Pérez-Pérez
et al., 2003 ), early modern humans ( Vandermeersch, 1981
; Sarig et al., 2016 ), and AMH ( Kaidonis
et al., 1992 ). The etiology of this modification is
not well known, but it seems to be due to
a multifactor combination of biomechanical, non-masticatory and dietary preferences (
Poisson et al., 2002 ; Egocheaga et al., 2004 ;
Estalrrich et al., 2011 ).
