The greater positive mechanical work in the single-support period in
chimpanzees is due primarily to greater positive power at the
hip, with small contributions from the knee and ankle. This
is consistent with previous assessments of sagittal plane hip moments,
which had indicated a substantial force requirement at the hip
during the stance phase in bipedal chimpanzees ( Sockol et
al., 2007 ; Pontzer et al., 2009 ). In middle
single-support, the lower back, pelvis, and lower limbs of humans
facilitate near-zero lower limb joint power, followed by a short
period in which mechanical energy is absorbed at the hip
and ankle (i.e., ‘preload’: Kuo et al., 2005 ; Zelik
et al., 2015 ).
