Human and bipedal chimpanzee joint moments are broadly similar in
direction and magnitude throughout limb swing, except in hip internal-external
rotation. This is consistent with greater circumduction of the hindlimb
in bipedal chimpanzee walking ( O'Neill et al., 2015 ).
The limb-swing rotational moments also provide some indirect support to
the inference of Stern and Larson (1993 :425) that the
obturator externus muscle is activated “to counteract [a] medial rotatory
effect” in the first half of limb swing, as there
is a net internal (i.e., medial) rotation moment at this
time in bipedal chimpanzees.
