In determining who would be affected, I followed an auditing
process recommended by Pence (2001) , wherein individuals who would
be located at a potential processing interchange had an opportunity
to suggest how their piece of the process could be
improved. Information accumulated through mapping was continually returned to the
oversight committee to discuss findings to date, which helped solidify
decisions about which interchanges were the most important to attend
to and which should be considered “out of scope,” similar
to how ethnographers move back and forth between field study
and institutional engagement ( Deener 2018 ). In the Findings
section, results of the mapping are discussed along with how
they were mobilized to produce improvements.
