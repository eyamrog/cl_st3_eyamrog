The results of this study suggest that aggression is an
effective mechanism via which hamadryas leader males establish and maintain
OMUs and accrue fitness benefits, but that such aggression may
not be advantageous in all contexts. The interindividual and intraindividual
variation we found among hamadryas males suggests that the tendency
to use aggression is likely not a ‘personality’ trait (
Clarke and Boinski, 1995 ; Capitanio, 1999 ) but that
each leader male is instead flexible in his use of
aggression, using more intense aggression when needed (e.g., toward females
in between-band takeovers) and limiting it in other contexts (e.g.,
toward other males).
