Changes can also be seen in the ocher assemblage from
Hohle Fels: the Aurignacian ochers are high in number (
n = 373) and show many textural and color varieties,
whereas in the Gravettian and Magdalenian, fine-grained, dark red, micaceous
hematite-rich ochers are the preferred type and were selectively collected
( Velliky et al., 2018 ). In addition, the Gravettian
and Magdalenian at Hohle Fels also show an increase in
modified ocher pieces, processing tools, and artifacts with intentional ocher
application, such as the painted limestone fragments from the Magdalenian
( Scheer, 1994 ; Conard and Floss, 1999 ; Floss
and Conard, 2001 ; Conard and Malina, 2010 , 2011
, 2012 , 2014 ). Currently, no ocher processing tools
are reported from the Aurignacian, and there is only one
reported anthropogenically modified ocher piece made of yellow ocher (no
traces of yellow pigment were observed on the beads). There
are currently no reports of hematite or ocher powder in
the cave sediments from Hohle Fels, although a so-called ‘Rötelschicht’
or ocher layer is reported from the nearby site of
Geißenklösterle ( Hahn, 1988 ). However, in general, it is
likely that microstratigraphic ocher contexts are overlooked or underreported.
