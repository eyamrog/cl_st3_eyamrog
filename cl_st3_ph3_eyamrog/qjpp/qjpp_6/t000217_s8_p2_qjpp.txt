It is thought that in humans, the specialization and training
of the motor cortex in one or both hemispheres allows
a better specialization and precision of the corresponding contralateral hand
(i.e. the left hemisphere controls the right hand because of
the decussation of the pyramidal tracts ; Vallortigara et al.,
1999 ; Cochet and Byrne, 2013 ). This accounts for
the bias of the individual. However, throughout human populations all
over the world, a rough ratio of nine right-handers to
one left-hander persists (e.g., Llaurens et al., 2009 ; McManus,
2004 ), and from archaeological evidence, we can assume that
it has been this way since up to 2 million
years ago ( Cashmore et al., 2008 ; Uomini, 2009
). This has led to the question as to why
and how this characteristically strong hand preference bias in a
population has been shaped by evolution.
