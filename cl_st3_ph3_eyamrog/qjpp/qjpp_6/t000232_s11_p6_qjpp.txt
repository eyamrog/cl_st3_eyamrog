The reality is that most eHealth projects ultimately fail (
Adler-Milstein, Bates, and Jha 2013 ; Leviss and Charney 2013
). Poor design, disorganized implementation, and inadequate attention to training
and change management can cause quality improvement (QI) projects such
as eHealth to make health care delivery more difficult instead
of less ( Dixon-Woods et al. 2014 ). As Grenier
and Bigdoli (2015) discovered in their evaluation of health reform
in Quebec, users will resist if the learning curve is
too steep, eHealth creates too much work burden, or there
are setbacks in implementation (see also Barr et al. 2017
; T. F. Bishop et al. 2013 ). This is
especially true when front-line workers feel they have little input
into system design ( S. Bishop and Waring 2016 ;
Grenier and Bidgoli 2015 ).
