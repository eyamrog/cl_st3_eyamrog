Here we comment only on the dose rates of samples
RB1 to 3, although the data for RB4 and 5
are also displayed on Table 5 . The K, U,
and Th contents decrease slightly with depth. The sedimentological study
suggests that the moisture history of the three samples is
not similar: RB1 has been maintained in high water table,
while RB2 and RB3 have been submitted to more variations
(see sections 4.1 Comprehensive view on the paleoenvironmental and chronological
contexts of the Middle Stone Age occurrence , 4.2 The
importance of the MIS 5 for the evolution of the
Middle Stone Age ). Meanwhile, the impact on the disequilibrium
of the U chain seems moderate ( Table 6 ).
The activity of the pre- 226 Ra part is in
slight deficit compared with the post- 226 Ra part for
RB1 (pre- 226 Ra to post- 226 Ra activity ratio:
0.78 + 0.05) while the ratio of these activities is
within 15% of unity for RB2 (0.87 ± 0.04) and
RB3 (1.14 ± 0.07). While the origin of this moderate
disequilibrium has not been determined, it can be estimated that
this issue (that would induce a ca. ±6% variation on
the dose rates depending on when the disequilibrium occurred) is
by far outweighed by the water and carbonate content issue.
A steady disequilibrium model is then used afterward. Table 5
