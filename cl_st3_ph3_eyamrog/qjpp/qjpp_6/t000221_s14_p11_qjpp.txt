For the chimpanzees, walking speed was calculated as the average
of the instantaneous forward velocity of four markers (i.e., 3
pelvis and 1 hip marker) over the full stride, whereas
for humans, a marker placed over the sacrum was used.
To account for differences in body size among subjects and
species, analyses were performed with dimensionless variables, using the base
units of body mass M b , gravitational acceleration g
, and average lower/hind limb length L . Lower/hind limb
length was measured as the average height of the greater
trochanter marker from the ground during quiet standing for humans
and during the middle of the single-support period for chimpanzees
(H = 0.92 ± 0.05, C = 0.39 ± 0.02
m). Velocity ( v ) was made dimensionless by the
divisor ( gL ) 0.5 and as the Froude number
( Fr = v 2 / gL ). Force was
made dimensionless by the divisor M b g , moment
and work by M b g L, and power by
M b g 1.5 L 0.5 . In all cases,
stance, swing, and stride duration were determined based on the
ground reaction forces.
