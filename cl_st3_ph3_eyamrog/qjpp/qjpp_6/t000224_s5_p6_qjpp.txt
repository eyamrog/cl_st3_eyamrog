Climatic and environmental similarities between nonhominin and hominin sites, especially
in MIS 11 (e.g., Marks Tey/Hoxne [Stratum D] and Barnham;
Table 2 , Table 10 ), suggest that the apparent
human absence at the former is due to factors other
than climatic tolerances or preferences ( Fig. 19 ). These
may include taphonomic and sampling issues, as previously discussed by
Ashton et al. (2006) with regard to fluvial and lacustrine
settings in MIS 11. Behavioral preferences linked to landscape features,
for example, the varying connectivity of lakes and rivers (
Ashton et al., 2006 ), may be a further factor,
although both types of landscape setting occur with and without
