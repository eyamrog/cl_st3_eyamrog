It can be expected that plants are more likely to
establish inside the cave or its entrance during periods of
reduced occupation by either animals or humans. Remains of these
plants in the cave deposits would likely be detected by
both pollen and sedaDNA. Comparison of the pollen and sedaDNA
records revealed six families and two genera in common. Of
the two genera ( Quercus and Artemisia ), Quercus may
be an unlikely pioneer plant of cave environments, but its
presence cannot be excluded. Artemisia species are described as preferring
no-shade to semi-shade conditions ( Fern, 1997 ). With the
cave opening towards the northeast, it enjoys only morning sun
and therefore is unlikely to have sustained Artemisia growth.
