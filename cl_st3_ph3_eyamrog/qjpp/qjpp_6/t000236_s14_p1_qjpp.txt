This study adopted a quantitative cross-sectional design, which allowed the
collection of quantified data from different categories of respondents (rural/urban
and nationalities; Creswell 2014 ). The study was conducted in
Ghana, Cameroon, Lesotho and Uganda, and these countries were randomly
selected from Western, Central, Southern and Eastern regions of Africa.
Contextually, the selected countries exhibit typical vulnerable African societies. Like
many other African countries, poor access to quality healthcare, poor
sanitation, high poverty and the accompanying high unemployment rate as
well as nutrition-related challenges, among others ( Nishio 2021 ;
WHO 2020b ; World Bank 2016 , 2018a , 2018b
), increase vulnerability and risk to health crisis, including COVID-19.
Specific to COVID-19 infection and deaths rates, most of the
selected countries had reported high infection and death rates (
WHO 2021b ). Again, like many other African countries, the
selected countries have limited capacity to vaccinate their citizens coupled
with high skepticism among citizens regarding the efficiency of vaccines.
