Recognizing the important role of the maxilla in hominin taxonomy
and phylogeny, as well as the need for an up-to-date
and comprehensive extant comparative framework, this study sets out to
evaluate maxillary morphology among modern humans and African apes. Included
in our analyses are substantial, sex-balanced samples of all genera,
species, and subspecies recognized in the current consensus view of
hominine taxonomy ( Table 1 ), noting that alternative interpretations
have been proposed. For instance, genetic studies have suggested that
Pan troglodytes verus should be elevated to full species rank
( Morin et al., 1994 ) and that a fourth
chimpanzee subspecies, Pan troglodytes ellioti , should be recognized (
Gonder et al., 1997 , 2011 ; Oates et al.,
