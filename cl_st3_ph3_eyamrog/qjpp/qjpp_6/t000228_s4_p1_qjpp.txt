Survival of identifiable plant remains is rare at Paleolithic sites.
Our current knowledge about human plant-use during this period is
defined and limited by the degradation and recovery of plant
remains and the availability of methods and technologies for plant
detection and identification. Caves can provide unique environments in which
the full effect of the elements on accumulated organic materials
may be mitigated. Using evidence from sedaDNA and pollen, we
were able to reconstruct the plant assemblages of the 15,000
yearlong sedimentary record of Aghitu-3 Cave.
