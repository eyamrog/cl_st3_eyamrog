Statistical analysis of the site-specific T min , T max,
and T range estimates for hominin and nonhominin British MIS
13 sites indicates no significant differences between the summer (U
= 3.0 [ n = 8], p = 0.250; Fig.
8 a), winter (U = 3.5 [ n = 7],
p = 0.400; Fig. 8 b), or range temperatures (U
= 7.5 [ n = 7], p = 0.629; Fig.
8 c), although the boxplots suggest slightly warmer summer and
winter temperatures at the nonhominin sites. While the sample is
small (and the provenance of a handaxe reported from the
Sidestrand till has been debated; Preece et al., 2009 ),
the results suggest that there was not a habitual preference
toward the warmest conditions in MIS 13, and that ‘cold
adaptations’ suitable for the subfreezing winter and cool summer conditions
at sites such as Happisburgh 1 and Waverley Wood were
already in place by late MIS 13 ( Fig. 8
