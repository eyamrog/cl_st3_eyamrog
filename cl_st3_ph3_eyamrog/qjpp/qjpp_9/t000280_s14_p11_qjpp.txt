The instances of these three amplifiers were exhaustively retrieved from
the corpus. Next, occurrences that were not instances of the
grammatical patterns in focus were removed (e.g., so used as
a linking adverb or very modifying a nominal or prepositional
head; the latter were very infrequent in the data). The
resulting data set ( so: N = 3510; very: N
= 4263; too: N = 1331) 8 was coded for
the variables: text (i.e., name of the text), text category
, year (year of publication), pos (word class of the
head word), and head (head word). The analysis focuses on
three perspectives: first, after determining the frequencies of the three
amplifiers per text, their distributions were compared across different text
categories of medical writing. Second, changes in the frequency over
the time period were traced using per-file-frequencies of the three
amplifiers grouped according to the text category . Finally, their
co-occurrence preferences with different adjective heads were compared using the
measures of “attraction” and “reliance” ( Schmid 2000 ).
