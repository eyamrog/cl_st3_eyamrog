As Table 5 shows, full merger is the most frequent
type ( N = 13) among the speakers, while the
unmerged type is the least frequent pattern ( N =
7). Upon inspecting the speakers’ gender, the unmerged category seems
more frequent among women, while the full merger type displays
the opposite. However, any generalizations based on such a limited
number of speakers should be treated with caution: all types
occur across different age groups and genders. Patterned differences between
groups of Scottish speakers may become more obvious when socio-economic
background is considered. The social class of the speakers included
in ICE Scotland is not available, however.
