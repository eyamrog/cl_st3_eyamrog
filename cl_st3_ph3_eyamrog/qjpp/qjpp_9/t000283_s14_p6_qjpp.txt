Using Praat, the F1 and F2 values of all target
vowels were extracted automatically in Bark ( Traunmüller 1990 ),
averaging across a section of the vowel that extended from
20-70 percent of its total duration. Based on Sönning and
Schützler (2018) , we then applied an adapted form of
Lobanov’s (1971) z-score normalization procedure. Based on the raw data,
we first determined the midpoint of the acoustic vowel space
for each speaker by calculating median values of F1 and
F2 for each of the eight normalizer categories for that
speaker, and by calculating their mean, again separately for each
formant. Next, the respective mean was subtracted from each individual
vowel measurement, creating values centered around zero. For all vowels,
these negative and positive values of F1 and F2—effectively deviations
(in Bark) from the midpoint of the space—were then divided
by the standard deviation of the sixteen centered median values
of normalizers for the respective speaker across both F1 and
F2. While the original Lobanov normalization procedure measures formants in
Hz and divides centered values by a standard deviation of
normalizers that is different for F1 and F2, using a
single standard deviation in both dimensions preserves relative positions and
distances between vowels and thus respects the psychoacoustic scaling introduced
by the Bark-scale.
