Grammaticalization refers to a specific kind of linguistic change “whereby
particular items become more grammatical through time” ( Hopper &
Traugott 2003 :2). As outlined by Heine (2003) , grammaticalization
is characterized by four key mechanisms: “erosion” (phonetic reduction), “decategorialization”
(loss of morpho-syntactic properties), “desemanticization” (semantic bleaching), and “extension” (context
expansion). This paper aims to evaluate the extent to which
these four mechanisms apply to negative tag variation in different
varieties of British English.
