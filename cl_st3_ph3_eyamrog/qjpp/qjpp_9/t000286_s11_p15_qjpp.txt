De Smet (2014 :85-86) found the earliest corpus attestations of
both transitive- need +ED and transitive- need +ING in the
beginning of the twentieth century. Despite the recent appearance of
the constructions in English, both are sufficiently established in Englishes
to be noted in grammars like Quirk, Greenbaum, Leech, and
Svartvik (1985 :1207) and Huddleston and Pullum (2002 :1206, 1245).
Quirk, Greenbaum, Leech, and Svartvik (1985 :1207) described transitive- need
+ED as “a raised object followed by an -ed participle
clause,” exemplified by the sentence reprinted here as (7). They
gave no indication that the construction is anything but standardly
available across Englishes. (7) I want / need this watch
repaired immediately. (Example [16.54ii] from Quirk, Greenbaum, Leech & Svartvik
1985 :1207)
