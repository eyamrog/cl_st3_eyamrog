As regards sound emission verbs, these also constitute an area
of the English lexicon that has undergone important changes (
Fanego 2017 ). With sound emission verbs (henceforth, clink verbs),
the sound results from the motion, that is, it is
not emitted via the vocal tract of an animate entity
(as is the case with laugh, sing, whistle , etc.).
Two examples of clink verbs are clink itself, as in
(9) above, and scream , as in (15); in both
cases the co-event conflated in the motion clause is concurrent
result, as defined earlier.
