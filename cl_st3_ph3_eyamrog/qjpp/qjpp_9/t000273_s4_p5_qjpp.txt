On the other hand, patients in the 1 year and
over group made greater use of present-tense verbs and framed
their experiences as ongoing, or at least as occurring in
the recent past but with results and effects that were
evident in the present. Rather than focusing on individuals, keywords
for these comments indicated a focus on entire teams and
departments, which is indicative of the wider focus of the
evaluation within this feedback, which tends to address multiple individuals
and departments, often over many years. The feedback from this
group also contained more autobiographical information, for example with patients
recounting their original diagnosis and providing details about how long
they had been visiting a particular provider. We have argued
that such information, which is not requested in the free-text
box sections of the feedback form, is foregrounded by patients
perhaps as a rhetorical strategy to bolster their credibility by
presenting themselves as experienced patients whose evaluations are based on
long-term experiences, and so to ultimately legitimate their perspectives and
the value of their feedback.
