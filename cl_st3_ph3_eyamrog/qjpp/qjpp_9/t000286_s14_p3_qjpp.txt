Tweets associated with a geographical area in Twitter data are
not assuredly representative of that area in the way that
dialectologists traditionally require. Twitter’s public search interface samples tweets based
on the physical location of the device that tweeted the
message, and also samples on the basis of locations that
users enter in their profiles. As such, an unknowable number
of tweets will be associated with locations where an author
did not acquire language as a child. Nevertheless, a working
assumption is that datasets built from Twitter are so large
that good data will suppress noise. Researchers have demonstrated that
geographically associated tweets can generate robust dialect maps (e.g., Eisenstein,
O’Connor, Smith & Xing 2012 ; Jones 2015 ; Pavalanathan
& Eisenstein 2015 ). I therefore report locations for tweets
but acknowledge the inherent noisiness of the data.
