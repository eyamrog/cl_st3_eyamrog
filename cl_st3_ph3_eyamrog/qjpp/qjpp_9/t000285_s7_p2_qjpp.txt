Negative tags, the variable of interest, consist of yes-no questions
that feature a negatively-marked auxiliary (with n’t ) and a
subject (personal pronoun or there ) which is attached to
a clause ( Quirk, Greenbaum, Leech & Svartvik 1985 :810).
In Standard English, the tag auxiliary agrees with the verb
in the clause it is appended to, called the “anchor”
or “anchor clause” ( Tottie & Hoffmann 2006 , 2009
; Pichler 2013 ), in person, number, and gender. There
is usually polarity reversal between the anchor and tag, such
that negative tags are typically attached to affirmative statements. In
this study, the variable is analyzed in terms of three
categories of variants which I refer to as “full,” “reduced,”
and “coalesced.” Full tag variants have canonical realizations, as shown
in (1), (2), and (3). (1) That’s stupid, isn’t it

though, doesn’t it ? (AA/613, Tyneside) (3) You’ve seen the
