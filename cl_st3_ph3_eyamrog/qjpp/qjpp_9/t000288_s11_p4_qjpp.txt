Previous accounts of N-is constructions differ in their views on
whether to treat them as transparent syntactic structures consisting of
a main and a subordinate clause (see Brenier & Michaelis
2005 ; Delahunty 2012 ) or as semi-fixed patterns (e.g.,
Biber, Johansson, Leech, Conrad & Finegan 1999 ; Carter &
McCarthy 2006 ; Aijmer 2007 ; Keizer 2013 , 2016
). The development of a pragmatic function presents an argument
in favor of the latter interpretation. That these constructions have
developed into DMs, which are, moreover, predominantly used in the
left periphery of the sentence, is also evidenced by the
different terms that are used for them: they are referred
to as “introductory constructions” ( Curzan 2012 ), “overtures” (
Biber, Johansson, Leech, Conrad & Finegan 1999 ), “projector/projecting constructions”
( Günthner 2008 ; Curzan 2012 ; Shibasaki 2014 ,
2015 ), “scope operators” ( Fiehler, Barden, Elstermann & Kraft
2004 ), “stance complement” constructions ( Quirk, Greenbaum, Leech &
Svartvik 1985 ), “utterance launchers” ( Schmid 2000 ), or
“focus formulas (with shell nouns)” ( Tuggy 1996 ; Tárnyiková
2018 ). The term “focalizer” is the one introduced by
Aijmer (2007) . Keizer (2013 :235) even claims that they
are “positionally constrained, always appearing in initial position.” Example (7)
is a serendipitous find from my own reading, which indicates
that they are also possible at the right periphery (see
also Traugott 2015 ).
