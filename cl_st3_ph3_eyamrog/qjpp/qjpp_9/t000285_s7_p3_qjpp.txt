Isn’t , doesn’t , and haven’t tags (in 1-3) are
given here as examples because they represent highly frequent verb
types, but other forms are included in the study, as
discussed in section 4 . Although different, “fuller” forms also
exist (e.g., is it not? ), these differ in their
syntax and are rare in vernacular speech compared to counterparts
with n’t (e.g., isn’t it ), so they do not
feature in this analysis. 2 Reduced variants are those where
the full forms have undergone attrition, namely the loss of
medial consonants and/or vowel reduction, as in (4), (5), and
(6). (4) There’s a song about it, int there ?
(James, Salford) (5) He likes his horse-riding, dunt he ?
