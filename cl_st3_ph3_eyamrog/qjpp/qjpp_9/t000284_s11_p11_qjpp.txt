The role of formant dynamics in the low-vowel distinction is
explicitly examined in Missouri ( Majors 2005 ) and Kentucky
( Irons 2007 ), both of which are outside the
LAGS area. Holt (2016) takes a different approach to analyze
whether there is regional variation in formant dynamics within North
Carolina. One analytic measure used was the spectral rate of
change, which measures how quickly formants change and how that
speed varies over the course of the duration of the
vowel ( Farrington, Kendall & Fridland 2018 ). Holt (2016)
finds that European American speakers realize goose and goat with
different spectral rates of change, with speakers in western North
Carolina having a lower rate of change (meaning more monophthongal
vowels) than speakers in eastern North Carolina. Furthermore, Holt (2018)
extracted measurements at multiple time points from these speakers to
analyze additional measurements like trajectory length, but a rigorous analysis
of the formant dynamics was lost for the back vowels
since the measurements from all timepoints were averaged together. Thus,
while researchers have acknowledged vowel trajectories in back vowels of
the American South, an in-depth analysis of them is lacking.
