By integrating these perspectives, the results shed light on different
aspects of the use of the amplifiers in focus. Frequency
analysis, which explored the differences across categories in terms of
the rates of occurrence, points to clear associations between specific
sub-registers—i.e., categories of eighteenth century medical writing—and their preference for
particular intensifiers. This in turn provides support for the idea
that, as in Present-Day English, the use of intensifiers in
eighteenth century medical writing is linked to situational characteristics and
communicative purposes of the sub-registers, which give rise to specific
intensifier + adjective/adverb combinations. The horizontal reading of individual examples
in turn shows how the situational context is linked to
specific linguistic features and their functions: what characterizes much of
eighteenth century medical discourse are physical descriptions of anatomy, physiology,
and surgical interventions and other and ways of treating illness,
and such passages offer frequent opportunities for using adjectives denoting
dimensions and physical properties. At the same time, as evidenced
by the large number of adjective types, it is clear
that intensifier + adjective/adverb combinations are used for a large
variety of meanings, many of which are specific to individual
texts and functions. This highlights the importance of considering register
and sub-register differences when analyzing amplifiers, and intensifiers more broadly.
