Emphasizing tags are sometimes called “attitudinal” ( Tottie & Hoffmann
2006 ; Pichler 2013 ) or “punctuational” ( Algeo 1990
), but the term “emphasizing” is adopted here to capture
the fact that speakers use these tags to emphasize a
point. Emphasizing tags are “self-centred” ( Algeo 1990 :446); they
are non-conducive as they do not elicit a response from
the interlocutor ( Coates 1996 :194; Tottie & Hoffmann 2006
:300). The speaker is fully committed to their proposition, and
these tags are not considered rude or antagonistic ( Algeo
1990 :446; Pichler 2013 :189). Example (21) illustrates the use
of an emphasizing tag, where Janet is talking about how
her dog previously had fleas and she takes precautions to
prevent that happening again. Moira replies that she has always
owned English Bull Terriers (a type of dog that has
short hair and does not require much grooming, unlike Janet’s
dog). The tag hanna (‘haven’t I’) emphasizes Moira’s point, and
she is fully committed to her statement. (21) Janet: So
now, what I do (.) I put Frontline on him
before he goes (.) I put Frontline on him the
day before (.) so he’s covered when he goes and
has his hair cut. Moira: Well I’ve always had English
Bulls, me, hanna ? (Salford)
