Property (c) (“the construction describes a single event”) is also
uncontroversial. As was discussed in section 3 in connection with
Talmy’s (2000) typology of event integration, VV ing OBL constitutes
a complex event that is conceptualized as unitary (and hence
represented as a single clause). Property (d) (“the verbs share
one or more arguments”) also seems to apply, since V
and V ing behave as a single unit, as described
in section 8.1, and thus share both the subject and
the oblique complement.
