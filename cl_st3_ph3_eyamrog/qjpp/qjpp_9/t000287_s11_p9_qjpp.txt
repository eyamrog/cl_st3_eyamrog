It is apparent, then, that when we say that mergers
cannot be reversed, we mean that two phonemes cannot merge
and then separate with the same sets of lexical items
associated with each phoneme. Garde’s Principle does not rule out
a subsequent split, whether into allophones based on a new
cue, or new phonemes. However, even under a conservative approach
to the concept of a merger reversal in which a
merger is reversed if and only if the same lexical
items are in their original categories, there appear to be
examples of such a reversal. There are three main explanations
