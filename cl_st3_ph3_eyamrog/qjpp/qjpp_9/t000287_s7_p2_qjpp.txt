One such apparent reversal is found in the English of
white speakers in St. Louis, Missouri. Here, a traditional urban
feature was the merger of /ɑr/ and /ɔr/, which the
community retreated from during the mid- to late-twentieth century (e.g.,
Murray 2002 ; Gordon & Majors 2006 ; Labov 2007
). In the following pages, I refer to these vowels,
using Wells’ (1982) lexical sets, as start (/ɑr/; e.g., barn
, card ) and north (/ɔr/; e.g., forty , cord
) respectively. These sets enable comparison of vowels across English
dialects even when they differ acoustically to a large degree.
