The implementation of the reversal offers independent evidence for Sloos’
(2018) account of perceptual salience playing a role in dialect
contact-induced sound change. Sloos (2018) finds that Austrian German moves
away from a merger of the bären and beeren vowels,
while Swiss German moves toward this merger. Both changes are
apparently due to contact with northern German varieties, which have
variable overlap of the vowels. Sloos (2018) suggests that Austrian
German speakers notice when northern German speakers produce a distinction,
because it is different from their own speech. As a
result, they target a distinction and separate the merger. The
opposite occurs for Swiss German speakers: they notice the overlap
as different from their own speech and target that overlap
as a prestige form.
