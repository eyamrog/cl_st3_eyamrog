While more research is of course needed to substantiate the
claim, it may be suggested that the two above-mentioned factors,
namely, (a) the use of size -clusters as a stylistic
resource (providing emphatic lexical variety) and (b) the sociocultural salience
of travelogues in earlier periods, may have decisively contributed to
the maintenance of size -clusters in the language (see the
comments in section 2 on the disappearance of accretive strategies)
and their further consolidation across genres: note, in this respect,
that travel writing is, diachronically—and stylistically—related to genres such as
fiction, diaries, or educational treatises ( McKeon 1987 ; Blanton
1997 ; Bohls 2005 ).
