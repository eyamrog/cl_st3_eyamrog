A relatively strong effect on the realization of the nurse
lexical set in SSE found in this study is the
following /r/ variant: when speakers produce an approximant /r/, the
preceding vowels are more mid-central and less distinct from each
other. When the following /r/ is realized as a tapped
or trilled variant, /ɪr/ and /ʌr/ are largely merged, whereas
a vocalized realization of /r/ does not promote the tendency
of /ɪr/-/ʌr/ merger and does not seem to pattern in
a meaningful way relative to the other two types. Moreover,
the realization of /ɪr/ is more strongly influenced by the
variable realizations of the following /r/ variant than /ʌr/ and
/ɛr/, which are less sensitive to the following phonetic contexts.
