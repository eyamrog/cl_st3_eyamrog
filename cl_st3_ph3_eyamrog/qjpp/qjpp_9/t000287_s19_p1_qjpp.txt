Understanding the implementation of the start - north reversal in
St. Louis involves three elements. The first consideration is whether
the overlap in the vowels constituted a complete merger or
not: is there any way to distinguish the vowels even
among speakers with a large amount of overlap? The second
and third considerations both concern potential effects of dialect contact.
The second consideration is to identify the phonetic path of
the reversal: in tracing this path, were there frequency and/or
orthographic effects on production? The third consideration is to determine
to what extent, if any, the reversal appears to be
socially motivated. I now explore each of these points in
turn.
