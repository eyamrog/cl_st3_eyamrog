We next studied the alkaline pH-dependent manner of Pep1 signaling
in vivo . We first examined the pH effect on
Pep1-induced RAM growth inhibition. Both an acidic pH (pH 4.7)
and treatment with the proton pump activator FC greatly attenuated
Pep1-induced RAM growth inhibition, suggesting that this process is dependent
on a relatively alkaline pH ( Figures 6 B and
6C). Given our abovementioned finding that an acidic pH also
enhanced RGF1 signaling, this attenuation may be attributed to a
combinatory effect of an acidic pH-induced inhibition of Pep1 signaling
and promotion of the RGF1 signaling. We then measured the
pH effect on the Pep1-induced Ca 2+ influx, an early
immune response marker using cytosolic Ca 2+ imaging. A [Ca
2+ ] cyt spike was observed after treatment with Pep1
at pH 5.7; however, the [Ca 2+ ] cyt spike
was less pronounced at pH 4.2 ( Figure 6 D),
suggesting that the Pep1-induced Ca 2+ influx is promoted by
a relatively alkaline pH. Furthermore, we measured the expression of
