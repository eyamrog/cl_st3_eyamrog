Since taxa could be used to classify a sample’s city
of origin, we next examined if AMR genes exhibited the
same stratification. A random forest model was trained (as above)
to predict city classification based on the mapped antimicrobial resistance
genes. While this model achieved 37.6% accuracy on held out
test data ( Figure S4 B), showing that it is
better than random chance (7.0%), the AMR profile was much
less accurate than the taxonomic predictor (88.0%). Since AMR genes
are more likely to be mobile, this is not surprising
and likely indicates that they represent weaker (but possible) city-specific
signatures.
