The 501Y.V2 variants of SARS-CoV-2 containing multiple mutations in spike
are now dominant in South Africa and are rapidly spreading
to other countries. Here, experiments with 18 pseudotyped viruses showed
that the 501Y.V2 variants do not confer increased infectivity in
multiple cell types except for murine ACE2-overexpressing cells, where a
substantial increase in infectivity was observed. Notably, the susceptibility of
the 501Y.V2 variants to 12 of 17 neutralizing monoclonal antibodies
was substantially diminished, and the neutralization ability of the sera
from convalescent patients and immunized mice was also reduced for
these variants. The neutralization resistance was mainly caused by E484K
and N501Y mutations in the receptor-binding domain of spike. The
enhanced infectivity in murine ACE2-overexpressing cells suggests the possibility of
spillover of the 501Y.V2 variants to mice. Moreover, the neutralization
resistance we detected for the 501Y.V2 variants suggests the potential
for compromised efficacy of monoclonal antibodies and vaccines.
