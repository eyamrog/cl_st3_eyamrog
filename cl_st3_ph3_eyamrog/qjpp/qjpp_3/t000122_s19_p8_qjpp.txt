We performed mutagenesis of the antibody CDRs to determine which
amino-acid residues were key for 1C3 interaction. Based on contacts
observed in the cryo-EM structure, eight amino-acid residue changes in
the CDRs and four changes in FRs were introduced and
evaluated for binding to GP ( Figure 4 A). Only
one of the single amino-acid residue changes, however—W111 A A
in CDRH3—substantially affects binding to GP. The side-chain nitrogen of
W111 A forms a hydrogen bond with the backbone carbonyl
of D117 on GP1, though the majority of the W111
A side chain is packed into a hydrophobic pocket of
the antibody itself. Thus, the W111 A change may alter
the conformation of CDRH3, as well as removing the hydrogen
bond. The observation that the majority of the single amino-acid
residue changes do not affect binding suggests the involvement of
multiple CDRs in the interaction; total binding energy could be
distributed more diffusely across each of the three different CDR–GP1
binding interfaces.
