Despite good separation of positive and negative controls (see STAR
Methods ) we identified several species in our negative controls
which were also identified as prominent taxa in the data-set
as a whole (See a core urban microbiome centers global
diversity ). Our dilemma was that a microbial species that
is common in the urban environment might also reasonably be
expected to be common in the lab environment. In general,
negative controls had lower k-mer complexity, fewer reads, and lower
post PCR Qubit scores than case samples and no major
flowcell specific species were observed. Similarly, positive control samples were
not heavily contaminated. These results suggest samples are high quality
but do not systematically exclude the possibility of contamination.
