Despite the attractiveness of liquid condensate formation as a mechanism
to explain chromatin packaging and condensation, the contribution of condensed
chromatin to the mechanical integrity of the nucleus and its
ability to respond to extranuclear forces applied to the nuclear
surface are difficult to reconcile with a liquid state. Two
groups have recently studied the contribution of chromatin organization to
mechanical stability of the nucleus. One found that the ability
of the nucleus to resist an applied force was reduced
approximately 10-fold by mildly digesting linker DNA and approximately 3-fold
by treating cells with histone deacetylase inhibitors ( Maeshima et
