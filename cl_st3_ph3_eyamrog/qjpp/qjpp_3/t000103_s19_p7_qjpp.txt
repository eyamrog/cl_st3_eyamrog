To identify critical interaction residues for neutralizing human anti-EEEV mAbs,
we generated an alanine-scanning mutagenesis library for the EEEV E2
glycoprotein and used it to map residues for which a
loss-of-binding phenotype of the antibody occurred. Similar to the competition-binding
analysis, three groups emerged. Critical alanine residues identified in this
analysis were mapped to the 4.2 Å cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM)
3D model of EEEV virus-like particle (VLP) (EMD-22276; PDB: 6XO4
; Figures S5 A–S5C). The antigenic sites correspond to epitopes
on domains A (EEEV-33), B (EEEV-7, EEEV-21, EEEV-97, EEEV-106, EEEV-129,
and EEEV-143), or A/B (EEEV-27, EEEV-93, and EEEV-94) of the
E2 glycoprotein ( Figure 3 D; Table S1 ). Variable
gene sequence analysis revealed EEEV-7 and EEEV-106 belong to a
common lineage, which is supported by their recognition of similar
residues for binding. Several mAbs (EEEV-27, EEEV-33, EEEV-93, EEEV-94, and
EEEV-147) also recognized critical residues in the arch 1 or
2 regions, an acid-sensitive β-connecter region that connects domain A
to domain B. Some E2 residues mutated to alanine were
identified as critical that are not surface exposed, which may
reflect allosteric effects on the epitope that impact antibody binding
( Table S1 ). Critical residues in the EEEV E2
domains were identified previously for murine anti-EEEV mAbs ( Kim
