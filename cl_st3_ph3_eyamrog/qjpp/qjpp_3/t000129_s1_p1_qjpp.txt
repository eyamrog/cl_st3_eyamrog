Regulatory landscapes drive complex developmental gene expression, but it remains
unclear how their integrity is maintained when incorporating novel genes
and functions during evolution. Here, we investigated how a placental
mammal-specific gene, Zfp42 , emerged in an ancient vertebrate topologically
associated domain (TAD) without adopting or disrupting the conserved expression
of its gene, Fat1 . In ESCs, physical TAD partitioning
separates Zfp42 and Fat1 with distinct local enhancers that drive
their independent expression. This separation is driven by chromatin activity
and not CTCF/cohesin. In contrast, in embryonic limbs, inactive Zfp42
shares Fat1 ’s intact TAD without responding to active Fat1
enhancers. However, neither Fat1 enhancer-incompatibility nor nuclear envelope-attachment account for
Zfp42 ’s unresponsiveness. Rather, Zfp42 ’s promoter is rendered inert
to enhancers by context-dependent DNA methylation. Thus, diverse mechanisms enabled
the integration of independent Zfp42 regulation in the Fat1 locus.
Critically, such regulatory complexity appears common in evolution as, genome
wide, most TADs contain multiple independently expressed genes.
