We focused on understanding how PTI-triggered extracellular alkalization is sensed
by RGF1 and Peps peptide-receptor complexes in the root meristem
zone. Additional studies into extracellular pH sensing by RGF-RGFR and
Pep-PEPR in other tissues and even other receptor-like kinase-mediated pH-sensing
processes will be a critical next step in further delineating
the mechanisms of plant extracellular pH perception. In addition, we
show that not only PTI-triggered alkalization but also altering medium
pH was sufficient to change RGF1 and Peps signaling response.
Thus, future studies of abiotic stress-triggered extracellular alkalization and plant-microbe
interaction-induced extracellular pH changes could be performed to verify our
findings. Another limitation of the study is the measurement of
absolute pH values in the apoplast. Although HPTS has been
used as a powerful pH indicator to track the relative
change of extracellular pH, the absolute pH values in the
apoplast cannot be directly read from HPTS fluorescence. Thus, further
technological advances are needed for accurately measuring extracellular pH.
