From these MAGs, we identified 838,532 CRISPR arrays, of which
3,245 could be annotated for specific CRISPR systems. The annotated
CRISPR arrays were mostly type 1-E and 1-F, but a
number of type II and III systems were identified as
well ( Figures 5 E and 5F), and some arrays
had unclear (ambiguous) type assignment. Critically, when we aligned spacers
to both our viral MAGs and all viral sequences in
RefSeq, the spacers in our identified CRISPR arrays closely matched
our predicted MAG-derived viruses. Moreover, while the total fraction of
spacers that could be mapped to our virus-containing MAGS and
RefSeq was similar (32.2% to our data versus 36.8% for
RefSeq), the mapping rate to our viral MAGs dramatically exceeded
the mapping rate to RefSeq ( Figure 5 C), which
provides additional evidence supporting the veracity of urban viruses.
