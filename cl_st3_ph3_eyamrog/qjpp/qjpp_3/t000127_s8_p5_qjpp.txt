In this study, we report that the extracellular pH can
be sensed by peptide receptors in the RAM. Using molecular,
biochemical, and genetic analyses, we found that the extracellular acidic
pH environment in the RAM is altered by the immunity-triggered
extracellular alkalinization. The extracellular pH change can be sensed by
RGF1 and Pep1 receptor complexes. The alkalinization attenuates the relatively
acidic pH-dependent RGF1-RGFR interaction to inhibit RGF1 signaling and the
proliferation of RAM. The alkalinization enhances the relatively alkaline pH-dependent
Pep1-PEPR interaction to promote immune response. The key pH-sensing residues
in RGF1 and PEPR1 are identified as sulfotyrosine and Glu/Asp,
respectively. Swapping the extracellular domains of RGI1/RGFR4 and PEPR1 can
switch the pH dependency of RAM development. Thus, our findings
uncover how plants sense extracellular pH signals in the RAM
to fine-tune growth and immunity.
