Interestingly, training markedly increased the number of neurons responsive to
both CS SU and sucrose (excitation, p = 2.03 ×
10 −10 , inhibition, p = 2.62 × 10 −5
, χ 2 test) ( Figure 2 F). Consistent with
this observation, population analysis revealed that training led to an
increase in similarity between the response to CS SU and
sucrose, quantified as a decrease in Mahalanobis distance between the
ensemble representations of the two stimuli ( Figure 2 G).
These results indicate that training fundamentally modifies the responsiveness of
aIC Fezf2 neurons, such that the response to the reward
cue becomes matched with that to the actual reward.
