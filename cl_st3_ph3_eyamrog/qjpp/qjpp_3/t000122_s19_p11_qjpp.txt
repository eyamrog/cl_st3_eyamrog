The broad specificity and neutralization capacity of 1C3 and 1C11,
coupled with their asymmetric binding footprints and likely mechanisms of
complementary interaction, suggested that they could together serve as an
effective therapeutic against EBOV and SUDV. We first evaluated the
protective efficacy of these antibodies individually in the laboratory mouse
model of EVD. Mice were treated with 1C3 or 1C11
24 h prior to exposure via the intraperitoneal (IP) route
with 100 plaque-forming units (PFU) of mouse-adapted EBOV. We chose
pre-treatment in the mouse model to confirm that the mAbs
had in vivo protective efficacy before moving to more stringent
post-infection treatment regimens in larger animal models. A human IgG1
antibody (specific for influenza A virus) was used as a
control. In mice, 1C3 and 1C11 provided 90%–100% protection from
death when administered at a dose of 25 μg or
higher, as compared to 10% survival in animals given no
treatment or given a control ( Figure 5 A).
