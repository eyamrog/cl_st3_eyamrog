In this study, we uncover that the signals triggering PTI
response could alter the acidic pH environment in the RAM,
which is essential for the cell division of RAM. We
further discover that extracellular pH change could be sensed by
RGF1 and Pep1 peptide signaling. The alkalinization of the RAM
apoplast inhibits the acid-dependent RGF1-RGI/RGFR interaction to inhibit RAM growth.
On the other hand, this alkalinization facilitates the relatively alkaline-dependent
Pep1-PEPR1 interaction to promote the immune response. Interestingly, swapping the
extracellular domains of RGI1/RGFR4 and PEPR1 switches the RAM growth
from a relatively acidic-pH-dependent manner to a relatively alkaline-pH-dependent manner.
These findings elucidate a mechanism that extracellular pH sensing in
plants is mediated by distinctive cell-surface peptide-receptor complexes. By modulating
the binding affinity of the peptide ligands to their cognate
receptors, the extracellular pH fine-tunes growth and immunity in the
RAM ( Figure 7 D).
