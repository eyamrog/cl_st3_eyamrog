We next sought to determine the fate of the daughter
cells. Tracking showed they not only migrated away from the
parent polykaryon, but they also fused with neighboring osteoclasts and
in some cases with each other ( Figures 4 A–4C;
Video S7 ). Thus, daughter cells were “recycled” into larger
polykaryons. This cellular recycling was also observed in vitro .
Recycling events were visualized by cell fate mapping trees (
Figure 4 C; Figure S1 ) and fission and fusion
of recycling cells lead us to name them “osteomorphs” to
distinguish them from osteoclasts.
