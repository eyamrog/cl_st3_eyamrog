Thirsty mice were trained to perform a reward-seeking task, in
which a sound (CS, 1 s in duration) predicted that
a 200-mM sucrose solution (US) would be available for the
mice to consume from a spout, but only if the
mice licked the spout during a response window (2 s
in duration) following CS presentation. Such trials were counted as
“hit” trials. To assess how changes in thirst state influence
behavioral and neural activity, we first conducted one session of
the imaging experiment on mice performing the task in a
thirsty state. Each mouse acquired 8 μl of the sucrose
solution in every hit trial. At the end of the
first session, we provided each of the mice with 2
mL of the sucrose solution. We then conducted a second
session of imaging on these mice (which were all quenched).
