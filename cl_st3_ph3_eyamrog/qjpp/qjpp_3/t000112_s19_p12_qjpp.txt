Given this strong signal from each city, we performed a
principal component analysis (PCA) on our taxonomic data, normalized by
the proportion of identified principal components (PCs) that were associated
with a metadata covariate (positive or negative). We hypothesized that
some principle covariates, such as climate, continent, and surface material,
might be prominent factors driving the taxonomic composition of a
given sample. We found that the two most prominent absolute
PCs associated strongly with the city climate (representing 28.0% and
15.7% of the variance of the original data, respectively), while
the continent and surface material associated less strongly ( Figure
2 B); the same trend held for the variation of
AMR genes ( Figures S3 E–S3G) as well.
