To provide more definitive evidence to support the finding that
extracellular pH is distinctively sensed by the RGF1 and Pep1
peptides binding to their cognate cell-surface receptors, we engineered a
chimeric receptor, PEPR1 LRR -RGI1/RGFR4 TM+KD , in which the
extracellular domain of RGI1/RGFR4 was replaced by that of PEPR1
( Figure 7 A). This chimeric receptor was expressed in
the pepr1/2 mutant driven by the native promoter of RGI1/RGFR4
, so that the Pep1 signal should only be recognized
by the chimeric receptor, which leads to the activation of
RGF1 signaling instead of Pep1 signaling. Upon treatment with Pep1,
the PEPR1 LRR -RGI1/RGFR4 TM+KD transgenic plants grown on MS
medium (pH 5.7) indeed showed a significant increase in RAM
size and meristematic cell number ( Figures 7 B and
7C), demonstrating that the chimeric receptor is functional. We then
examined the pH response of the transgenic plant. If our
findings regarding the pH-sensing mechanism hold true, we would expect
a switched pH dependency during RAM growth. Intriguingly, the pH
dependency of RAM growth in the transgenic plant was switched
from an acidic pH-dependent manner to a relatively alkaline-dependent manner
( Figures 7 B and 7C). This result confirmed that
(1) the interaction of Pep1-PEPR1 is relatively alkaline dependent; (2)
the acidic-dependent RAM growth is mediated by RGF1 signaling; and
(3) the pH dependency is determined by the extracellular domain
rather than the intracellular domains or downstream signaling steps. Thus,
this domain-swapping assay provides compelling evidence to support our findings
of the extracellular pH-sensing mechanism and regulation in the RAM.
