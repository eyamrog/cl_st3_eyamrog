In many ways, risk communication likely needs to be tailored
to a community's perceived risk and experiences rather than relying
on universal scientific assessments ( 14 , 35 , 132
). However, emotional attachments to place, while important determinants of
how one perceives risk and experiences stress and anxiety, are
seldom found in risk communication and policies in response to
risk and hazards ( 143 ). Rollason et al. (
145 ) suggest that a rethinking of risk communication is
needed, focusing specifically on participatory approaches where local community members
coproduce more effective types of local risk communication with experts
and policy decision makers.
