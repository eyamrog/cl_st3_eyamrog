Disaster research emerged within the academic fields of geography, sociology,
and other social sciences in the 1950s and 1960s, focused
mostly on hazards of the natural environment ( 33 )
and loss and damages from extreme weather events ( 92
). Over the course of the 1990s, a paradigm shift
took place changing scholarly focus from studies of specific hazards
to attempts to understand the dynamism and context of disaster
( 178 ). Specifically, this work tries to understand what
kinds of risk and distributional impacts are produced from interactions
between the physical and social environments ( 24 ), the
conditions that create place-based vulnerabilities for communities ( 45 ),
and ways to improve the capacities of communities to cope
with disaster ( 44 ).
