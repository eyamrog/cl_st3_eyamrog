For both males and females, the age-standardised mortality rates for
all cancers combined are projected to continuously decline over the
period 2015–19 to 2040–44 ( figure 1C, D ), by
21·5% (95% UI –36·0 to –1·1) for males from 106·3
to 83·4 (95% UI 68·0 to 105·1) per 100 000
males, and by 20·6% (95% UI –35·0 to 0·1) for
females from 73·4 to 58·3 (95% UI 47·7 to 73·5)
per 100 000 females ( appendix pp 25–26 ). The
mortality rates for age groups aged younger than 60 years
showed a steady decline from the 1980s, and the decline
in the mortality rate started 10–20 years later for age
groups 60 years and older ( figure 1C, D ).
Due to population growth and ageing, the actual numbers of
cancer deaths are projected to increase by 36·4% (95% UI
15·3 to 63·9) for males, from 132 440 in 2015–19
to 180 663 (95% UI 152 719 to 217 126)
in 2040–44, and by 36·6% (95% UI 15·8 to 64·1)
for females, from 102 103 in 2015–19 to 139 482
(95% UI 118 186 to 167 527) in 2040–44 (
appendix pp 25–26 ).
