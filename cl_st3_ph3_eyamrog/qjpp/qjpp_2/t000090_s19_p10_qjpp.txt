As a final test of whether Snt1 phosphorylation could trigger
[ ESI + ], we generated single phosphomimetic (S/T→D) variants
at annotated phosphorylation sites within Snt1. We episomally expressed the
mutant proteins for ~25 generations, eliminated the expression vector by
counterselection, and propagated them for another ~25 generations ( Figure
2 E). This propagation diluted the cytoplasm by ~10 7
-fold, minimizing the potential effect of any remaining phosphomimic protein.
Transient ancestral exposure to very low amounts of wild-type Snt1
or an empty vector control did not induce [ ESI
+ ] and neither did a phosphomimetic mutation (S481D) at
the sole site within Snt1’s ordered domain ( Figure 2
F). In contrast, transient ancestral expression of phosphomimetic variants at
multiple sites within Snt1’s disordered regions gave rise to heritable
zinc resistance ( Figure 2 F). Induction of the trait
was not due to differences in Snt1 abundance, as the
variants were expressed similarly ( Figure S2 C). Although other
physiological circumstances might also promote [ ESI + ] induction,
our results suggest that Snt1 phosphorylation linked to G2/M spindle
checkpoint arrest by nocodazole can trigger the prion.
