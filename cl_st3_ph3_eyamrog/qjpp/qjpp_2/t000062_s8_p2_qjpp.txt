The challenges of modelling future patterns in cancer incidence and
mortality have been further complicated by the COVID-19 pandemic. Although
the direct impacts and outcomes of the pandemic remain to
be determined, several changes in health-care delivery occurred as a
result of efforts to control COVID-19 outbreaks. The most apparent
effects include the severe delays in cancer detection, diagnosis, and
treatment, which have been reported in a number of countries.
For example, in Australia, non-urgent elective surgery was temporarily suspended,
and there was a reduction in cancer diagnostic and surgical
procedures related to colorectal and other cancers during March to
May, 2020. Colonoscopies and sigmoidoscopies decreased by 55% from March
to April, 2020, and numbers of colorectal surgeries in May
2020 were 16% lower than those recorded for March 2020.
The paucity of data on health-care disruptions has resulted in
early exploratory modelling of hypothetical scenarios for treatment delays, although
none of the existing studies examined the potential long-term impact
of treatment delays on cancer mortality in Australia.
