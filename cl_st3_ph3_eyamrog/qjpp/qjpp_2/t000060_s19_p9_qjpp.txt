Analysis stratified by household dwelling type showed pronounced negative effects
on MHI-5 scores among females who lived in flats or
apartments (−4·1 points [–5·4 to –2·8]) or in semi-detached houses,
terraced houses, or townhouses (−4·8 points [–6·4 to –3·2]) at
the time of the lockdown whereas a smaller negative effect
was seen in females living in separate houses (−1·7 points
[–1·9 to –1·4]; figure 2C ; appendix p 11 ).
In males, a negative effect of lockdown was also observed
for those living in flats or apartments (−2·1 points [–3·2
to –1·0]) but not for those living in semi-detached houses,
terraced houses, or townhouses (0·1 points [–1·6 to 1·8]). A
negative effect was also seen for males living in separate
houses (−0·6 points [–1·0 to –0·3]). However, effects were not
significantly different between dwelling types among males.
