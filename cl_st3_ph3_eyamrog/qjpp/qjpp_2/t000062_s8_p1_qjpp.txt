Cancer control programmes implemented today will have an effect on
the burden of cancer over future decades. Long-term projections of
national cancer incidence and mortality therefore provide essential information for
policy makers, helping to inform health service planning and public
health policies. There are many factors which influence future patterns
of cancer incidence and mortality, including population growth and ageing,
changes in exposure to risk factors, and developments in cancer
prevention, diagnosis, and treatment. This diverse range of influential factors
makes projecting future incidence and mortality rates for all cancers
combined very complex, so it is important that appropriate projection
methods are carefully selected, and that attempts are made to
account for these factors whenever possible. Several different statistical models
have been developed and used to project future cancer incidence
and mortality rates, ranging from a simple assumption of a
constant cancer mortality rate to more complex models such as
age-period-cohort (APC) models and other generalised linear models (GLMs) that
account for changes in exposure to risk factors. An overview
of statistical projection methods for all cancers is provided in
the appendix (pp 6–7) .
