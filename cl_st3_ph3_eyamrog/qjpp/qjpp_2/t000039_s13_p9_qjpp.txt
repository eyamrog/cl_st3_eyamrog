The needs of children experiencing homelessness have only recently drawn
attention and coincide with the commitment by the US Interagency
Council on Homelessness to end family homelessness by 2020 (
77 ). Despite this lofty goal, the problem continues to
grow because it is layered and multifactorial, involving inadequate units
of affordable housing, insufficient availability of vouchers, family separation, food
insecurity, and violence. In a single year, 97% of these
children move up to 3 times, 40% attend at least
two schools, and 33% experience a child protection investigation (
28 ).
