Although heterogeneity was high across individual studies ( I 2
=98%; p<0·001)—study-specific risk ratios varied between 1·04 and 5·77—all risk
ratios indicated elevated rates of mortality in adults who, as
children, had experienced care ( figure 2 ). However, for
two studies (three samples) from Finland, the increased risk attributable
to care exposure was marginal. Pooling of effect estimates revealed
that adults with experience of previous state care had around
twice the risk of mortality than those without (risk ratio
2·21 [95% CI 1·62–3·02]).
