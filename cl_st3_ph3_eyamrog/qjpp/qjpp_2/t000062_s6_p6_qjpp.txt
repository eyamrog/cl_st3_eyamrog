This study provides previously unavailable information on projections of cancer
incidence and mortality in Australia to 2044, for all cancers
combined as well as for 21 individual cancer types. These
projections can help inform health service planning to meet the
requirements for future cancer care in Australia. These results can
also serve as benchmarks against which to measure the impact
of future cancer prevention and intervention initiatives, and we have
illustrated a method to use the baseline projections as a
benchmark to assess the impact of different hypothetical or real
scenarios of delayed treatment during the COVID-19 pandemic. Despite the
continuous decline in cancer mortality rates, and the decline or
plateau in the incidence rates, our projections suggest an overall
51% increase in the number of new cancer cases and
a 36% increase in the number of cancer deaths over
the 25-year projection period. This means that continued efforts to
increase screening uptake and to control risk factors, including smoking
exposure, obesity, physical inactivity, alcohol use, and infections, must remain
public health priorities.
