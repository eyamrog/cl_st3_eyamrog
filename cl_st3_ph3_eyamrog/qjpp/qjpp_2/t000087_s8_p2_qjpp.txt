The treatment approach in children and adolescents diverges from that
in adults in terms of risk classification, conventional chemotherapy regimens,
the application of response as assessed on F-fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG)–positron-emission tomography
(PET) and computed tomography (CT) to subsequent modifications to therapy
and the use of consolidative radiation therapy. Trials involving children
also include second malignant neoplasms in the primary end point
of event-free survival. In contrast to the chemotherapy regimen of
doxorubicin, bleomycin, vinblastine, and dacarbazine (ABVD) that is used in
adults with Hodgkin’s lymphoma, regimens in children with advanced-stage disease
include etoposide and cyclophosphamide with continued use of glucocorticoids, while
limiting cumulative doses of doxorubicin and bleomycin.
