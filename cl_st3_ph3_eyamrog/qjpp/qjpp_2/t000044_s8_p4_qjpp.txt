Evaluating the prevalence and burden of TBI in individuals who
are homeless or marginally housed is critical to understanding the
unique challenges and health-care needs of this population. Furthermore, identifying
factors that contribute to heterogeneity across studies is integral to
establishing standardised approaches for future research and in finding targets
for the prevention of TBI and treatment of its sequelae
in this population. In this study, we aimed to estimate
the lifetime prevalence of TBI in homeless and marginally housed
individuals, to quantitatively evaluate factors that moderate estimates of prevalence,
and to systematically review the association between TBI and health
and functioning-related outcome measures in this population.
