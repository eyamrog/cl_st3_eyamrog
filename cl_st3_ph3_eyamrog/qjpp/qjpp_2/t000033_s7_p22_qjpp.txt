Climate change literature has now begun to question the role
of place attachment in influencing risk perception and decision-making within
hazard-prone areas of coastal communities ( 56 ). Owing to
the subjectivity of well-being, researchers have started to use place
attachment as an alternative concept to measure the well-being of
communities affected by climate change. Investigators use qualitative surveys that
ask participants to define the value they assign to particular
places ( 180 ), and they use a mixed-methods approach
that combines surveys with statistical analyses to understand the correlation
between place attachment and flood risk ( 18 , 128
).
