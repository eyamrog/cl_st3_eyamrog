Cystic fibrosis results from biallelic mutations in the CFTR gene.
More than 1000 pathogenic CFTR mutations have been identified. Processing
and trafficking mutations (e.g., Phe508del , the most common CFTR
mutation) reduce the quantity of CFTR on the cell surface,
and channel-gating defects (e.g., Gly551Asp and other CFTR gating mutations)
limit anion transport. CFTR mutations that result in lesser impairment
of CFTR protein activity, collectively defined as residual function mutations,
have also been identified. Most patients with gating or residual
function CFTR mutations are heterozygous for the Phe508del mutation.
