Permanent housing is essential for ensuring the health, nutrition, well-being,
and educational achievement of children. More than one-quarter (11.2 million)
of the 43.8 million renter households are extremely low income
and on the verge of homelessness ( 54 ). The
common standard of affordability is 30% of a household's income,
yet only an estimated 7.5 million rental homes are affordable,
leaving a shortage of at least 3.7 million affordable units
( 54 ). In recent years, this situation has worsened.
According to the National Low Income Housing Coalition, “The severe
shortage of affordable homes for extremely low-income renters is systemic,
affecting every state and metropolitan area” ( 54 , p.
11). Extremely low-income households is defined as at or below
30% of area median income. Families with “worst case housing
needs” who spend more than 50% of their income on
rent are at extremely high risk of becoming homeless and
have limited resources for other necessities such as heat, food,
and health care ( 2 , 72 ).
