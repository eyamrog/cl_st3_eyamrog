Despite treatment advances in multiple myeloma, relapses are common. No
standard of care has been established for patients who have
disease progression despite receiving the three main classes of myeloma
therapy (immunomodulatory agents, proteasome inhibitors, and anti-CD38 antibodies), and outcomes
are poor, with infrequent complete responses, a median progression-free survival
of 3 to 4 months, and a median overall survival
of 8 to 9 months. Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)–modified T
cells are a promising new treatment, and CD19-directed CAR T
cells are approved for B-cell cancers. The B-cell maturation antigen
(BCMA)–directed CAR T cell idecabtagene vicleucel (ide-cel, also called bb2121)
showed promising efficacy in a phase 1 study involving patients
with relapsed or refractory myeloma. These results prompted a pivotal
study (KarMMa) to assess the efficacy and safety of ide-cel
in patients with triple-class-exposed relapsed and refractory myeloma and to
evaluate pharmacokinetics, immunogenicity, and potential biomarkers for response and progression.
