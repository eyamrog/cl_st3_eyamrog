The number of refugee youth worldwide receives international attention and
is a top priority in both academic and political agendas.
This article adopts a critical eye in summarizing current epidemiological
knowledge of refugee youth mental health as well as interventions
aimed to prevent or reduce mental health problems among children
and adolescents in both high- and low-to-middle-income countries. We highlight
current challenges and limitations of extant literature and present potential
opportunities and recommendations in refugee child psychiatric epidemiology and mental
health services research for moving forward. In light of the
mounting xenophobic sentiments we are presently witnessing across societies, we
argue that, as a first step, all epidemiological and intervention
research should advocate for social justice to guarantee the safety
of and respect for the basic human rights of all
refugee populations during their journey and resettlement. A constructive dialogue
between scholars and policy makers is warranted.
