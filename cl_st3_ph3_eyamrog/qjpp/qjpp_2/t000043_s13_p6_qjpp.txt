In addition to research on the Kentucky plans, research on
Oregon's innovative approach to managed care has also considered changes
in access. In 2012, Oregon transitioned to a global budgets
model, in which coordinated care organizations (CCOs) accepted full financial
risk for their enrollees. This change was expected to improve
access by increasing CCO incentives to provide timely preventive care.
Indeed, in a prepost treatment analysis of pregnant women, Oregon's
coordinated care model showed improved access to timely prenatal care
( 39 ).
