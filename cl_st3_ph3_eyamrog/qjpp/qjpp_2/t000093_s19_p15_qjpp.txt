It is conceivable that multivalent NTF2-associated RBDs are unique in
engaging SG RNPs to form a condensed network that coexists
with that of PBs. However, the RBD of FXR1, a
dimeric RBP that interacts with UBAP2L but not NTF2 (
Figure 2 D), mimics G3BP RBD in all assays (
Figures 5 B–5E and S5 E–S5J). Remarkably, use of a
large panel of additional Corelets (n = 25) indicates that
high v RBD is sufficient for PB-studded, polyA+ SG assembly,
irrespective of whether the RBD is folded (RRM) or unfolded
(RGG), from an SG or PB protein, or linked to
G3BP IDR ( Figures 5 F–5I and S5 E–S5G). Despite
this RBD interchangeability, we surmise that RBD-RNA specificity and relative
interaction strengths contribute to the lack of relationship between type
and number of RNA-binding motifs and relative phase thresholds (
Figures 5 C and 5F–5H). Importantly, Corelets are capable of
plugging into non-SG interaction networks, as those of DCP1A—a PB
protein with PPIs but no RBD—recruit PB but not SG
markers ( Figure 5 J). Thus, polyA+ SGs with attached
PBs are the “default” multiphase condensate encoded by high-valence RBD
nodes ( Figure 5 K).
