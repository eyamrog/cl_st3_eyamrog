Findings: Health gains (expressed in HALYs per 1000 people) ranged
from 127 (95% uncertainty interval 96–167; undiscounted) for the 8%
junk food tax and 212 (102–297) for the fruit and
vegetable subsidy, up to 361 (275–474) for the saturated fat
tax, 375 (272–508) for the salt tax, and 581 (429–792)
for the sugar tax. Health expenditure savings across the remaining
lifespan per capita (at a 3% discount rate) ranged from
US$492 (334–694) for the junk food tax to $2164 (1472–3122)
for the sugar tax.
