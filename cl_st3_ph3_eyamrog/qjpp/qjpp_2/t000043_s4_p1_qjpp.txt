In the years since Sparer's review, the literature on Medicaid
managed care has grown to reflect the design of modern
managed-care programs. In particular, researchers have made considerable gains in
empirically evaluating managed-care programs administering benefits to high-risk patient groups.
Studies from different states and on different high-risk populations suggested
that quality of care can improve under managed care, but
substantial caveats remain. Transitioning high-risk enrollees to managed care in
itself can have implications for the quality of care (
16 ). Also, the variability in results across high-risk groups
cannot be disentangled from differences in state-specific programs, also noted
by Sparer. More work using national data or additional states
is needed to generalize findings and additional analysis exploring the
efficiency effects of including high-risk populations.
