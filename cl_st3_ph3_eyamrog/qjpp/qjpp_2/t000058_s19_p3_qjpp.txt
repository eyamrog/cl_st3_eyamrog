From 2002 to 2019, a decline in life expectancy was
more probable than an increase in 124 mostly urban MSOAs
of 6791 (1·8% of all MSOAs) for women, with posterior
probabilities of greater than 80% that these were true declines
in 34 of these. The largest estimated decline of 3·0
years (95% CrI 0·9–5·3; posterior probability of the estimated decline
being a true decline 99·6%) occurred in an MSOA in
Leeds ( figure 3B, C ). Elsewhere, median posterior change
was positive, ranging from less than 1 year in 408
MSOAs to more than 7 years in 63 MSOAs. Posterior
probability of an increase in male life expectancy was more
probable than a decrease in all but one MSOA in
Blackpool, in which life expectancy changed by −0·4 years (−2·3
to 1·6; posterior probability of being a true decline 64%).
For the other MSOAs, the increase ranged from less than
1 year in 31 MSOAs to more than 7 years
in 114 MSOAs. The largest increases in female and male
life expectancies were seen in some MSOAs in and around
London (eg, in the London Borough of Camden). In 5133
(75·6%) MSOAs, male life expectancy increased more than female life
expectancy ( figure 4 ), leading to a closing of
the life expectancy gap between female and male sexes.
