Although health-care spending in the UK has been less affected
by austerity than other sectors, the annual 1·3% increase since
2010 is about a third of the long-term average, and
insufficient to keep up with the increasing demand of an
ageing population. The imbalance between funding and demand has led
to longer waiting times for primary and specialist care, with
the greatest effects in deprived areas. The real-term cuts in
public health spending have also been larger in the north
and northeast of England, where life expectancy lags. Smoking cessation
and health checks, which affect diseases with substantial contribution to
mortality inequalities, had larger than average funding cuts.
