Central to a life course approach is a focus on
time, in terms of being explicit about the temporal order
of exposure to risk factors and the mapping of these
exposures onto developmental periods ( 5 , 43 ). The
temporal order of exposures is important because it helps explain
the causal process by which exposures lead to disease risk
( 43 ). The application of a developmental framework is
also critical because individuals may be more vulnerable to the
negative impact of exposures during certain phases of childhood; in
addition, early-childhood exposures can have consequences on developmental processes that
go unnoticed until youth are older or become young adults
( 35 , 82 ).
