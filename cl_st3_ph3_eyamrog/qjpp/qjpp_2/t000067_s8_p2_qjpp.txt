Inflammation and oxidative stress are central components of the pathogenesis
of acute kidney injury, implicating multiple subtypes of immune cells.
Evidence of a pathway linking the bone marrow to kidney
injury has emerged, involving soluble urokinase plasminogen activator receptor (suPAR)
— the circulating form of a glycosyl-phosphatidylinositol–anchored three-domain membrane protein.
This receptor is normally expressed at very low levels on
a variety of cells, including endothelial cells, podocytes, and, with
induced expression, immunologically active cells such as monocytes and lymphocytes.
Levels of suPAR are strongly predictive of progressive decline in
kidney function. Long-term exposure to elevated suPAR levels directly affects
the kidneys by means of pathologic activation of αvβ3 integrin
expressed in podocytes, resulting in proteinuria. Whether suPAR has an
effect on kidney tubular cells — the cells most affected
in acute kidney injury — is unclear.
