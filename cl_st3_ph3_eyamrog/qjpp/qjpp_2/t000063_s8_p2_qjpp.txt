Compared with other people who are incarcerated, those with dual
disorders have more serious criminal histories, including previous incarcerations and
violent offences, and higher rates of serious institutional misconduct while
in prison. Dual disorders are more common among women than
among men and among survivors of childhood physical and sexual
abuse who are involved in the criminal justice system, and
they are associated with physical health problems and impairments in
psychosocial, cognitive, and occupational functioning. After release from prison, individuals
with dual disorders have a substantially higher risk of reincarceration,
attempting suicide, and hospitalisation due to injuries than do those
without. Furthermore, having a co-occurring substance use disorder can worsen
the prognosis and increase the severity of symptoms of mental
illnesses, and is associated with poorer treatment responses and adherence
to medication for mental illness. Dual disorders pose substantial challenges
to treatment planning, and criminal justice systems are often not
equipped to address these complexities.
