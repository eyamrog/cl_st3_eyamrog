Overall, our results and those from other prospective studies suggest
that social isolation itself has little or no direct effect
on the risk of developing coronary heart disease or stroke.
Such findings are contrary to a common public health message
that social isolation increases the risk of developing vascular disease.
However, we found that social isolation, in particular living alone,
was associated with a higher risk of first coronary heart
disease or stroke events being fatal before reaching hospital, perhaps
because affected individuals have no one immediately available who can
help in responding to these acute illnesses.
