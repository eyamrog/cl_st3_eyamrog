Compared with that of many other federal agencies, HUD's definition
excludes substantial numbers of families ( 53 , 68 ).
This definition drives HUD's annual Point-in-Time (PIT) count of people
who are homeless as well as funding allocation. The PIT
count serves as the official number reported in HUD's Annual
Homeless Assessment Report (AHAR) to Congress. Less restrictive definitions of
homelessness used by other federal agencies arose from the McKinney–Vento
Homeless Assistance Act, passed in 1987 as the first major
federal response to homelessness. McKinney–Vento was reauthorized in 2009 as
the Homeless Emergency Assistance and Rapid Transition to Housing (HEARTH)
Act and goes beyond the HUD definition to include (Pub.
L. 111–22 §1003)
