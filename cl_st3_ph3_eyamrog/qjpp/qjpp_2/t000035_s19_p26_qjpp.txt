The studies that we classified as having weak study designs
with a high risk of bias were mostly cross-sectional regression
analyses that identified associations but could not tease out the
direction of causality and could not address confounding due to
omitted variables such as unmeasured aspects of health status. Some
of the studies included a rich set of regression controls
to minimize this risk, but the potential for some unmeasured
confounding remained. Despite this caveat, results across these cross-sectional studies
were fairly consistent in finding that informal care in the
presence of formal care improved care recipient outcomes. In most
cases, informal care was measured as a binary variable indicating
the presence or absence of an informal caregiver.
