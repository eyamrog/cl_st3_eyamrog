For each of the 21 cancer types (16 separately for
females and males, plus five sex-specific sites) and the remaining
cancer types grouped as other cancers, the selection of the
most appropriate statistical projection model for each cancer type was
based on the Bayesian Information Criterion and model validation using
observed data ( appendix pp 12–21 ). GLM and APC
models were used to incorporate smoking exposure as a covariate
for lung cancer, PSA testing rates as a covariate for
prostate cancer, and participation rates in breast cancer screening as
a covariate for female breast cancer. There were strong associations
between the cancer rates and corresponding factors, and the inclusion
of these factors resulted in improved performance in the model
validation. Standard APC models or age-stratified APC models were used
to project incidence and mortality rates for each remaining cancer
type. APC models include age, period, and cohort components within
the framework of a GLM with Poisson distribution ( appendix
p 16 ). APC models are flexible and can implicitly
reflect changes in risk factor exposures based on past trends
in cancer rates. In particular, the period and cohort effects
in APC models can generally represent exposure to a range
of risk factors, including environmental and behavioural cancer risk factors.
To project incidence and mortality rates beyond the observed period
for cancers other than prostate and lung, future periods and
cohorts were assumed to have the same effect as those
for the most recently observed period and cohort, with a
damping factor used to reduce the drift by 8% each
year following the last observation. For prostate cancer incidence we
used period-specific PSA testing rates to predict the future period
effects. For lung cancer incidence and mortality, we used cohort-specific
cigarette tar exposure to predict the future cohort effects (
appendix pp 16–19 ). We defined the uncertainty interval (UI)
as the confidence interval of the estimates based on the
variance of the parameters in the model. To project rates
for all cancers combined, the total numbers of cases and
deaths were obtained by summing the estimated numbers of new
cases and deaths for the 21 individual cancer types and
the other cancers group.
