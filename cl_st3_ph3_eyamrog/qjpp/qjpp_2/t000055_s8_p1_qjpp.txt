In early December, 2020, a phylogenetically distinct cluster of SARS-CoV-2
was genetically characterised in the southeast of England. Most cases
had been detected in November, with a small number detected
as early as September, 2020. Genomic surveillance revealed that this
new variant, termed B.1.1.7, has several mutations of immunological significance
and has been spreading rapidly, with cases increasing in frequency.
It is important to understand how these mutations could affect
the presentation and spread of COVID-19 so that effective public
health responses can be formulated.
