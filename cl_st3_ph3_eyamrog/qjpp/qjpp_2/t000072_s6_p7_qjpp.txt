The results from our study underscore the profound effect that
marked weight loss can have on metabolic function in people
with diabetes. The similar findings in participants in the two
groups challenge the current belief that upper gastrointestinal bypass has
clinically meaningful effects on key metabolic factors involved in glucose
homeostasis and the pathogenesis of diabetes that are independent of
weight loss. However, the difficulty in achieving successful long-term weight
loss with lifestyle therapy often renders gastric bypass surgery far
more effective than diet therapy for most patients with obesity
and type 2 diabetes.
