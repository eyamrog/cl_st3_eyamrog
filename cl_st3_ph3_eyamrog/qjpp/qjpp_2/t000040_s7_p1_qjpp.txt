Globally, more people are living in urban than in rural
areas, with 55% of the world's population residing in urban
areas in 2018 ( 77 ). The urban environment shapes
populations through multiple health pathways, such as physical activity, air
pollution, green spaces, social capital, access to health services, and
transport, among others ( 60 , 89 ). Urban transportation
policies, in particular, have been suggested as a principal pathway
through which urban environments can either impair or promote health
( 23 , 60 ). This field is dynamic and
rapidly evolving; recent advances in technology have led to transportation
innovations such as multimodal integration via online applications, on-demand digitally
enabled transportation, electrification of motorized transportation, and connected autonomous vehicles
(AVs) ( 26 ).
