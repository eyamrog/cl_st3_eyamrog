In analyses of individuals without missing data in the 1958
and 1970 birth cohort studies, we ascertained whether the proportional
hazards assumption had been violated by computing Schoenfeld residuals. With
no strong evidence that this was the case, we computed
hazard ratios with accompanying 95% CIs for care and total
(or suicide) mortality separately for each study. Next, we pooled
the results from the analyses of raw data alongside published
study-specific estimates using a random-effects meta-analysis, an approach that incorporates
the heterogeneity of effects in the computation of their aggregation.
An I 2 statistic was computed to summarise the heterogeneity
in estimates across studies. Lastly, we explored the magnitude of
the care–mortality association according to different contexts, including sex, study
quality, region, and complexity of covariate adjustment. All analyses were
conducted using Stata, version 15.
