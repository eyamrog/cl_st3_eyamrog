Like Study 1, we find support for Hypothesis 1: those
who were more (vs. less) open reported better mental health,
b = 0.149, SE = 0.051, p = .005, 95
% CI [0.047, 0.251]. In addition to this direct effect,
openness had both beneficial and costly indirect effects on mental
health through community integration, perception of both group and personal
discrimination and, to some extent, identity importance.
