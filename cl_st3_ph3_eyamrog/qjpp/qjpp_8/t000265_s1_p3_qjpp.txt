Results: Our results show that increasing deductibles reduced the probability
of mental health care use at the transition to adulthood
by 13.6% for females (−13.6%, CI 95%: −22.1%, −5.2%), and
by 5.3% for males (−5.3%, CI 95%: −11.8%, 1.2%). The
reduction was larger among females in the lowest (−18.9%, CI
95%: −35.4%, −2.3%) and second lowest (−21.3%, CI 95%: –36.7%,
−5.9%) income quartiles. Additionally, we find increased treatment cessation in
high deductible years to happen across all levels of mental
health care need.
