Not only the type and level of support, also the
characteristics of the people who provide it can vary substantially,
with different types of social contacts “specializing” in different types
of support ( Wellman and Wortley, 1990 ; Vacca, 2018
). For instance, one might prefer to go to concerts
with their friends, seek job-related information from colleagues, but ask
parents for help with childcare. Migrants are no exception to
this differentiation of supportive resources, in that they maintain multiple
and specialized ties they can rely on for support in
different domains ( Bilecen and Sienkiewicz, 2015 ; Bilecen and
Cardona, 2018 ; Lubbers et al., 2010 ; Menjívar, 2000
; Vacca et al., 2021 ).
