Our main study population consists of 1,541,210 individuals. Table 2
shows that the study population is characterized by slightly more
males (51.0%) and one fifth of first- or second-generation migrants.
The proportion of adolescents that used mental health services at
17 is higher for females than males (8.8% vs 7.3%).
Specialist care was more frequently received than basic care, and
the latter was more frequent for females (2.8% comparing to
1.4%). Gender differences are also observed in the proportion of
adolescents using any psychotropic medicine (3.4% for females and 5.3%
for males) and the type of drug classes used. From
those using only one class of psychotropic drugs, males were
almost three times more likely to use ADHD agents, while
anxiolytics and antidepressants are twice more common among females. The
characterization of the subpopulation of mental health care users at
17 is provided in eTable 2 in appendix E .
