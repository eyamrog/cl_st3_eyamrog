Intensity of depression was measured using the Center for Epidemiologic
Studies Depression Scale Revised (CES-D-R 10), a self-report measure composed
of 10 items assessing symptoms of depression in the last
week. The CES-D-R 10 was derived from the original 20-item
CES-D ( Radloff 1977 ). It has been revised to
better capture general dysphoria and reflects primary symptoms of a
major depressive episode ( Eaton et al., 2004 ). Later,
the instrument was validated for the measurement of depression (
Van Dam and Earleywine 2011 ). The CES-D-R 10 has
been validated in South Africa among Zulu, Xhosa and Afrikaans
populations ( Baron et al., 2017 ). The CES-D-R 10
is constituted of three items on depressive mood (feel depressed,
fearful and lonely), five items on somatic complaints (bothered by
things, trouble with focus, trouble with activity, restless sleep and
lack of energy) and two on positive feelings (positive feeling
for the future and happiness). Each item ranges from “rarely
or none of the time” (score of 0) to “all
of the time” (score of 3). The total score —between
0 and 30— is calculated by adding the 10 items.
Any respondent scoring 10 or above is considered depressed with
the intensity of depression rising with the score. The internal
consistency in the present study was 0.79.
