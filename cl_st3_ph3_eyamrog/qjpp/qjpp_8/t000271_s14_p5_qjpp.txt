As it turned out, the authors involved in the TRJPS
corpus were predominantly based at universities in the USA (often
Ivy League universities), as opposed to a great diversity evident
among the authors contributing to the PJPS corpus. Unlike in
some previous studies (e.g., Xia et al. 2015 ; Shen
and Björk 2015 ) which found that the majority of
authors publishing in predatory journals come from developing countries, institutions
in both developing and developed countries were involved in the
present study. In fact, over 40 per cent of the
articles in the PJPS corpus were produced by authors based
at various institutions from North American and European countries, with
the USA topping the list (24 out of 110). The
top two developing countries involved were China (10) and Nigeria
(7) (see Soler and Wang, 2019 for more details). Note
that where the authors were based does not tell us
anything about their first language (L1) backgrounds. There may well
be native and non-native speakers of English in both groups
of authors. Both corpora have single- and co-authored articles, although
the top-ranking journal has more co-authored articles than single-authored ones
(65 vs. 45), whereas the contrary is true for the
predatory journal articles (74 single-authored vs 36 co-authored).
