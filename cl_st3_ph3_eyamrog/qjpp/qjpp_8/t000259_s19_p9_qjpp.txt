Interestingly, the results provide only mixed evidence in support of
H4 and H5. Whereas respondents are more likely than not
(53%) to intend to vaccinate if presented with a vaccine
developed in 9 months, a 12 month timetable offers only
a modest increase in intention (54%) which fails to attain
statistical significance (β = 0.00, p > 0.10); inconsistent with
H4. However, and consistent with H4, respondents indicated significantly higher
intentions to vaccinate (57%; β = 0.02, p < 0.05)
when presented with candidates that spend more than a year
(15 months) in development.
