Additionally, vaccine candidates will vary in their potential effectiveness at
preventing people from contracting the virus. Although we cannot know
candidates’ potential effectiveness until clinical trials conclude and are replicated
by the FDA, we do know that the US has
set a 50% minimum effectiveness threshold in order for vaccines
to be disseminated ( FDA, 2020 ), and that (encoruagingly)
early late-stage clinical trial data suggest effectiveness of greater than
90% for leading vaccine candidates (e.g., Grady 2020 ). Previous
research suggests Americans will be more likely to intend to
vaccinate if they perceive greater health benefits from doing so
(e.g., Nan and Kim 2014 ). Consequently, I hypothesize that
Americans will prefer more-effective vaccine candidates to those that are
less effective ( H2 ).
