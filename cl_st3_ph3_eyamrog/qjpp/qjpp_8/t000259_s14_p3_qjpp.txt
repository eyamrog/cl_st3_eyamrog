I test these expectations using a conjoint experimental design (
Luce and Tukey 1964 ; Strezhnev et al., 2013 ;
Hainmueller et al., 2014 ). Conjoint experiments are useful for
testing hypotheses where the number of varied dimensions (“attributes”) and
the different values they can take on (“levels”) would create
a prohibitively large design. That is, if we were to
divide up the sample and randomly assign just one treatment
per respondent, the number of possible assignments would approach or
even exceed the number of respondents participating in the survey
(a “small cell” problem).
