Academic discourse represents a type of “socially situated practice” (
Hyland 2012 : 60), and as noted by Wells (1992)
, each academic discipline constitutes a way of making sense
of human experience that has evolved over generations and, as
a result, each has formed its own particular practices and
modes of discourse. In other words, academic communication depends, to
a large extent, on shared experience and conceptual fluency, thereby
making the use of formulaic language particularly relevant ( Kecskes
2010 ). Indeed, empirical observations have shown that academic writing
is marked by a high degree of formulaicity and fixedness
of usage patterns (e.g., Pérez-Llantada 2014 ; Wang, 2018 ).
