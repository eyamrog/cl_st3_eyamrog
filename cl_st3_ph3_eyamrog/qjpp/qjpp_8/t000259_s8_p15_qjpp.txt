Conceivably, Americans might be more reluctant to try vaccines that
have never successfully been brought to market in the past
(i.e., mRNA), or that feature a version of the virus
that they may worry could get them sick (i.e., attenuated
vector vaccines). However, as many Americans lack even basic knowledge
about viral transmission and immunity ( Miller, 1998 ; Winneg
et al., 2018 ), it is unclear whether or not
they might draw meaningful distinctions between these types of vaccines.
Consequently, in lieu of a formal hypothesis, I propose the
following research question: Does the procedure by which vaccines aim
to produce an immune response influence Americans’ preference for some
vaccines over others? ( RQ1 )
