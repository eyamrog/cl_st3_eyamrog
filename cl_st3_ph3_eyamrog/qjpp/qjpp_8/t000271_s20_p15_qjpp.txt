The wide variety of usage patterns makes a marked contrast
with the formulaic pattern we find that in the TRJPS
corpus. Admittedly, as different journals may have different stylistic guidelines
as regards the choice of tense and voice, the seemingly
regular usage pattern as observed in the TRJPS corpus may
simply be a result of the journal's style guide. In
that sense, the lack of such a pattern in the
predatory journal articles may not necessarily mean insufficient experience or
unawareness of disciplinary conventions on the writers’ part, but rather
the lack of relevant requirements from the journal. However, according
to our expert informants, that is unlikely to be the
case. They recognised the usage pattern in the TRJPS articles
and interpreted it as a way for authors in their
field to take responsibility for what they write and claim.
While this may well be a convention that is specific
to the discipline, which the authors of the PJPS articles
seemed not fully aware of, more journals need to be
involved in future research to substantiate or refute the claim.
