Despite the negative consequences of stigma, there is scant research
into disability - related stigma and its association with depression
and self-esteem. Research is often limited to some types of
disabilities such as developmental or learning disabilities and focuses on
High Income Countries ( Ali et al., 2015b ; Chan
et al., 2017 ; Zhang et al., 2014 ). Studies
in Low or Middle Income Countries (LMICs) have investigated subjective
well-being or quality of life of persons with disabilities (
Uppal 2006 ), or the public stigma of disability and
access to services ( Mulumba et al., 2014 ; Parsons
et al., 2015 ). Very few studies have engaged with
disability, stigma and mental distress such as anxiety and depression
( Trani et al., 2016 ) and none have sought
to investigate the connection to self-esteem. Similarly, no studies have
investigated the connection between depression associated with a disability and
public stigma.
