At the same time, evidence indicates that alongside these benefits
there can be costs to having a stronger group identification,
particularly for members of stigmatized social groups (for an overview,
see Jetten et al., 2017 ). In this vein, findings
specific to sexual minorities are mixed. While some researchers find
positive relationships between identification and well-being among sexual minorities (
Doyle and Molix, 2014 ; Fingerhut et al., 2010 ;
Bourguignon et al., 2020 ), others do not. Begeny and
Huo (2017) , for example, showed that sexual minority identification
has certain indirect costs for mental health, because it heightens
minorities’ vigilance (and thus perceptions of) the group-based discrimination around
them (in line with evidence found in other stigmatized group
contexts; Begeny and Huo, 2018 ; Leach et al., 2010
; Operario and Fiske, 2001 ; Sellers and Shelton, 2003
).
