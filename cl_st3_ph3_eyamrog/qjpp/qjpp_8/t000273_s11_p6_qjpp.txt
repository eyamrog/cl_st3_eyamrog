Recent research has begun to address this previous lack of
linguistic focus. For example, Baker et al. (2019) and Brookes
and Baker (2017) analysed 29 million words of written patient
feedback on NHS services in England. With the help of
corpus linguistic methods, these studies were able to account for
a larger and more representative set of patient comments than
previous studies had, while the analyses moved beyond simply describing
the content of the comments to consider the linguistic choices,
and attendant discourses and rhetorical strategies, that patients drew upon
in order to represent and evaluate their healthcare experiences. These
studies provided detailed understanding of the language that patients use
in their feedback, including how this language is shaped by
social and technological factors (see also: ( Baker and Brookes,
2021 ; Brookes and McEnery, 2017 , 2020 ).
