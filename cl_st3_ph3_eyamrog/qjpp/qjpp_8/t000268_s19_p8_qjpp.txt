When estimating the models for the different groups of countries
according to their income level, we obtain similar results to
those obtained by region. First, there is a difference in
the coefficient sign of the uncertainty indicator: it is negative
when it enters the model contemporaneously and positive when it
is introduced with a one-period lag. Second, there is a
difference in magnitude, the effect of the uncertainty shock lagged
one period being always greater. The largest impacts are obtained
for low-income countries. Again, these results support the idea that
the bulk of the impact of a global uncertainty shock
is not necessarily reflected immediately in the suicide rate. This
indicates that there is a significant but complex relationship between
both variables and that this can evolve over time.
