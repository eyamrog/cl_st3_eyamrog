The SWIDM is a network formation and disease spread model
for a closed population of autonomous agents (nodes) that can
create infection-relevant relationships (edges). Networking decisions are based on the
trade-off between the social benefits (e.g., sense of belonging, affection),
costs (e.g., time, effort, money), and (potential) harm of infections
(e.g., symptoms, absence from work, hospitalization) that a tie has
for an agent. Agents seek to myopically maximize personal utility
by (i) forming ties to other agents (requires the consent
of the opposite agent), (ii) maintaining existing ties to other
agents, and (iii) dissolving ties to other agents (unilateral decision).
Decisions on these actions are based on an agent's position
in the network, individual risk perception related to the disease,
and the distribution of disease states among agents in the
network (see section Utility).
