In summary, the three SDs discussed here all have a
direct bearing on the CRF and infection rate variables. They
are also significantly connected to patterns of social hierarchy and
degrees of social marginalisation and exclusion observed across different parts
of the world. In this sense, incorporating these (and potentially
other) SDs into epidemiological modelling can help elucidate the interaction
between health outcomes and other forms of social policy, including
the health implications of various social welfare regimes.
