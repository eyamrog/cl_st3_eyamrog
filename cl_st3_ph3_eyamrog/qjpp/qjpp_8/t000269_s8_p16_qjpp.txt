Heinrich (2014) writes that parents' (and especially mothers') work is
not unequivocally beneficial for children. Benefits accrue from working parents
being positive role models and from more income improving children's
lives. However, disbenefits derive from low-quality and insecure jobs, such
as parental stress and poorer child wellbeing ( Heinrich, 2014
). Further, low-income parents are more likely to work in
insecure, low-quality jobs and their children are more likely to
experience suboptimal childcare or go unsupervised completely ( Heinrich, 2014
).
