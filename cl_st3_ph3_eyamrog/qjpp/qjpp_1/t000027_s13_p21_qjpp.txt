Finally, governments may act to set the price of selected
products to protect and promote public health goals. For example,
a minimum pricing policy for alcohol in Scotland was associated
with an increase in purchase price of alcohol and a
reduction in weekly purchases of alcohol ( 99 ). Many
governments have also offered subsidies, discounts, or free distribution of
healthy products such as fruits and vegetables, thus reducing the
cost incentives for less healthy, often highly processed foods.
