Some of the shortcomings of monitoring and assessment tools may
have hampered their effective use in policy making. The literature
has highlighted, in particular, issues with unequal spatial coverage (Global
South in particular); insufficient attention to vulnerable populations; lack of
dynamic indicators; insufficient set of risks and benefits assessed; and
lack of clarity in definitions and goals of tools. To
improve the use and effectiveness of assessment tools, authors have
recommended that researchers should strive to harmonize assessment methods, standardize
data collection methods, and assess impacts of inaction and economic
impacts of health cobenefits. Perhaps more fundamentally, current researchers are
urged to work toward the establishment of causation and to
improve understanding of decision-making processes and of public acceptability of
solutions ( 27 – 30 , 55 ).
