Large-scale human genetics is an increasingly powerful source of data
for the discovery and validation of therapeutic targets in humans
. pLoF variants, predicted to largely or entirely abolish the
function of affected alleles, are a particularly informative class of
genetic variation. Such variants are natural models for lifelong organism-wide
inhibition of the target gene and can provide information about
both the efficacy and safety of a candidate target .
However, pLoF variants are rare in human populations and are
also enriched for both sequencing and annotation artefacts . As
such, leveraging pLoF variation in drug target assessment typically requires
very large collections of genetically and phenotypically characterized individuals, combined
with deep curation of the target gene and candidate variants
. Although previous studies of pLoF variants in LRRK2 have
found no association with risk of PD , no study
has assessed their broader phenotypic consequences.
