In the DPP trial, although metformin was overall less effective
than lifestyle modification, it was as effective as lifestyle modification
in prediabetic participants with BMI ≥35 kg/m 2 ( 56
) but less effective than placebo in those aged ≥60
years (44% diabetes risk reduction among participants aged 25–44 years
versus 11% in those ≥60 years of age) ( 56
). It is worth noting that analyses in these subgroups
(obesity, younger age) were post hoc and not prespecified. Subsequent
analyses also demonstrated that, among prediabetic women with a history
of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), metformin and intensive lifestyle modification
had equivalent effects on the incidence of diabetes (50% risk
reduction) ( 9 , 88 ). After 15 years of
follow-up in the DPPOS, the effect of metformin (versus placebo)
was greater among women with a history of GDM (41%
risk reduction) as compared with women without a history of
GDM (6% risk reduction) ( 29 ).
