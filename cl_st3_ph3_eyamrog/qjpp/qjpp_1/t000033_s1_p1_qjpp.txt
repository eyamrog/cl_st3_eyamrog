With accelerating climate change, US coastal communities are experiencing increased
flood risk intensity, resulting from accelerated sea level rise and
stronger storms. These conditions place pressure on municipalities and local
residents to consider a range of new disaster risk reduction
programs, climate resilience initiatives, and in some cases transformative adaptation
strategies (e.g., managed retreat and relocation from highly vulnerable, low-elevation
locations). Researchers have increasingly understood that these climate risks and
adaptation actions have significant impacts on the quality of life,
well-being, and mental health of urban coastal residents. We explore
these relationships and define conditions under which adaptation practices will
affect communities and residents. Specifically, we assess climate and environmental
stressors, community change, and well-being by utilizing the growing climate
change literature and the parallel social science literature on risk
and hazards, environmental psychology, and urban geography work, heretofore not
widely integrated into work on climate adaptation.
