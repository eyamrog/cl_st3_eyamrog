To date, the decisions on institutional and home-based formal care
coverage in the United States have been based on the
relative costs of these programs. At the individual level, using
informal care or formal care is also likely driven, at
least in part, by the perceived costs of each. Yet
we still do not know the relative benefits, or the
benefits of working in tandem, in order to make better
decisions on the basis of cost–benefit trade-offs at the individual
and national levels.
