To address larger environmental problems caused by the food system,
including agriculture's contribution to climate change, soil depletion, water consumption,
and pesticide and synthetic fertilizer use, cities will need to
extend the reach of urban food policy beyond their municipal
borders. One way is for cities to use their political
power to advocate for national policies to address food system
sustainability and resilience. Another approach is to leverage collective purchasing
power to buy food that meets social and environmental goals
and, in so doing, to try to shift industry practices.
Several US cities, beginning with Los Angeles in 2012, adopted
food procurement standards developed by the nongovernmental organization Center for
Good Food Purchasing, which support five values: local economies; environmental
sustainability; a valued workforce; animal welfare; and health and nutrition
( 91 ). Adopting a good food purchasing policy enabled
the Los Angeles (California) school district to increase the purchase
of locally grown produce from 9% to more than 50%,
to switch to antibiotic-free chicken, and to reduce meat purchasing
by 15%. The policy also prompted the city of Oakland
(California) school district to shift some food purchases from meat
to plant-based proteins ( 62 ).
