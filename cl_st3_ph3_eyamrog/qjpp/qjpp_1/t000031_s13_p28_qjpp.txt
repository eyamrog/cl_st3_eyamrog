In the Da Qing study, which was conducted from 1986
to 1992 and enrolled individuals with IGT in China, diabetes
prevention was associated with a significant reduction in diabetes during
the 6-year trial period ( 81 ) and in the
posttrial period after 20, 23, and 30 years of follow-up
( 42 , 62 , 63 ). In addition, the
lifestyle arm was associated with a decreased number of deaths
from cardiovascular disease and a reduction in all-cause mortality after
23 years ( 62 ). In the Da Qing study,
after 30 years of follow-up, in addition to the reduction
in deaths from cardiovascular disease, there was also a significant
36% reduction in cardiovascular disease events associated with lifestyle modification
( 42 ). The Da Qing study also showed that
diabetes prevention through lifestyle modification can affect microvascular complications, with
a 47% lower risk of severe retinopathy in the intervention
group than in the control group over a 20-year period
( 41 ).
