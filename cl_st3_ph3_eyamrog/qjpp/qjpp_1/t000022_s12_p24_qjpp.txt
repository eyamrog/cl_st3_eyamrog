Much of the appeal of big data comes from its
high volume attribute, which carries the aspiration of statistical flexibility
to support complex analyses and protect against type I and
II errors. High volume, referring to the number of participants,
may lead to an aspiration of generalizability, in which identified
statistical associations are broadly applicable to other populations. However, a
high volume of participants cannot replace the representativeness of participants,
and no quantity of data can confer generalizability if representativeness
is lacking ( 72 ).
