We next consider the probability that variants found in chronic
infections are transmissible. In most of our patients (15/27), we
observed either S:E484K/Q and/or deletions at S:Y144, both of which
are highly recurrent mutations in the global phylogeny. However, globally
circulating variants bearing these mutations were inferred to be less
transmissible than variants bearing other highly recurrent mutations (Table 2
and ref. ). On the other hand, several mutations associated
with higher transmissibility (for example, S:P681R and ORF1a:∆3,675–3,677) were never
observed in the chronic infections herein (Table 2 ).
