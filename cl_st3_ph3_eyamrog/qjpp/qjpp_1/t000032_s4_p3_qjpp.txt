The urgency of decision-making based on scientific evidence can exist
in a variety of situations, not just on the minute-by-minute
timescale of a worldwide pandemic. Many regulatory decisions in environmental
health have to be made on the basis of only
a handful of studies. Often, public health officials and policy
makers cannot wait years for another large cohort study to
replicate (or not) existing findings. In such situations where decisions
need to be made, the more code and data that
can be made available to assess the evidence, the more
informed those decisions will be. In the interim, follow-up studies
can be conducted and revisions to the evidence base can
be made in the future if needed. The reanalyses of
the Six Cities Study and the ACS Study provide a
clear example of this process, and history has shown those
results to be highly consistent across a range of replication
studies.
