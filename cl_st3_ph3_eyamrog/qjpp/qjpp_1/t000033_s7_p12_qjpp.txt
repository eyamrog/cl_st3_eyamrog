Displacement of communities has been studied across the social sciences
through the lens of place identity—the sense of belonging to
a specific place, which can be mediated by dynamic city
environments and their associated stressors ( 104 ). Place identity
can take longer to establish than emotional bonds (i.e., place
attachment). This condition is especially relevant to coastal communities where
population mobility creates a community of permanent and seasonal residents
( 136 ). In relation to risk of flooding and
SLR (see Supplemental Figure 1 ) in coastal communities, the
social sciences literature has focused specifically on place attachment to
describe how communities and residents ascribe meaning to place, which
can influence their reaction to changes in the urban environment
and community structure under conditions of stress (e.g., gentrification, transformative
adaptation) ( 3 , 133 ).
