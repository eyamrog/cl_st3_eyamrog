To test the functional significance of this increased insolubility of
FUS and other RBPs, we looked in the mutant animals
for evidence of aberrant splicing of their target pre-mRNAs, similar
to that which we described previously in brain and spinal
cord tissue from C9orf72-positive and gene-negative patients with ALS/FTD .
As in those cases, RBP insolubility in the Δ14/WT and
P517L/WT animals was also associated with alteration in splicing. For
example, analysis of two targets of hnRNP H showed decreased
inclusion of ATXN2 exon 24 and increased inclusion of hnRNPDL
exon 8—consistent with decreased hnRNP H activity—in Δ14/WT and P517L/WT
animals compared to wild-type controls (Fig. 2g ). P517L/WT showed
a greater magnitude of detergent insolubility and splicing alterations compared
to Δ14/WT animals (Fig. 2d,g and Extended Data Fig. 4d
), once again suggesting increased toxicity of P517L over Δ14.
