Combining both types of care was superior to either informal
care or formal care alone for depression ( 20 )
and physical health ( 59 ). Informal care alone was
superior for mortality ( 45 ). Formal care was superior
for loneliness ( 72 ). No differences by caregiver type
or combination for unmet needs ( 73 ) or mental
health ( 59 ) were found. Ayalon ( 7 )
differentiated the effects by care provider type and found that
live-in migrant formal care providers are less likely to be
associated with neglect than live-out formal care providers.
