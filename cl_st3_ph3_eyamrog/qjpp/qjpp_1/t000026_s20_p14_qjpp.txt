Clark et al. ( 15 ) argue that research that
fails to embrace complexity is of limited use to policy
makers who need to juggle complex real-world problems and priorities
in their everyday lives. As delivery, impacts, and effectiveness of
policies are, to a certain extent, context dependent, policy makers
may be justified to consider as irrelevant an evidence base
generated elsewhere ( 15 ). To be of use, research
must include understandings of real-world conditions and of the complex
environment in which decision makers operate. Research that will help
embrace complexity, integrate underlying systemic drivers of CC, and simulate
possible impacts of interventions will help frame and explain solutions
so as to increase their public acceptance ( 8 ,
55 ).
