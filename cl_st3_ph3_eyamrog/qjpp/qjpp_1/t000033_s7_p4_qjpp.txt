The mix of increasing climate risk and complex and growing
population conditions in coastal communities brings the potential for more
debate regarding coastal development and the future habitability of at-risk,
near-shore sites and the well-being of their residents. More than
a decade ago, scholarly writing began to appear that described
the potential psychological effects of heightened climate change impacts on
coastal communities and their inhabitants. This literature presented opinions on
and scenario-based assessments of the expected economic and psychological challenges
that communities will face with accelerating SLR, increased flooding, and
inundation. A central theme of this research was on the
loss of sense of place and associated psychological stress.
