Of the 525 patient specimens evaluated by mCARMEN, only 2
specimens had no detectable levels of RNase P above threshold,
1 of which was positive for SARS-CoV-2 while the other
was virus-negative. The RNase P-negative, but virus-positive, specimen likely had
a high concentration of viral RNA, which sequestered amplification materials
during the reaction, limiting RNase P amplification. The double-negative specimen
suggested possible extraction failure or sample integrity issues and was
thus excluded from further analysis.
