Giving temporary cash assistance is different from tackling structural socioeconomic
and racial inequity. We cannot eradicate health inequity without addressing
socioeconomic and racial inequity. Eradication requires mitigating, resisting, or undoing
the structural influences that adversely shape health ( 39 ).
Williams & Collins ( 126 ) argued that reparations are
essential to eliminate segregation's negative effects and are likely to
dramatically reduce racial differences in health. As structural racism continues
to claim a prominent place in US public discourse, one
option may be to incorporate reparations and similar historical remedies
into cash-transfer policy designs.
