So how have mHealth initiatives, designed to increase access, autonomy,
and consumer choice in health, benefited populations in LMICs? To
date, advances in and benefits from an increased investment in
mHealth products have favored both product design and capacity development
in HICs, most notably countries in Europe and the United
States. Evidence described earlier in this chapter is a testament
to the growing place of mHealth as a core public
health tool, with COVID-19 forcing the hand of digitally reticent
governments and health organizations to update and upskill ( 11
). Yet, evidence of scaled-up initiatives being accessible to the
poorest is lacking. LMICs typically face additional challenges with building
and retaining the technical capabilities necessary to drive and sustain
mHealth ( 62 ). Access to training and retention of
qualified staff remain chronic issues in LMICs. The Pacific Islands
region's attempt to stymie the flow of qualified workers to
New Zealand and Australia is futile in the face of
ever-increasing demands for services.
