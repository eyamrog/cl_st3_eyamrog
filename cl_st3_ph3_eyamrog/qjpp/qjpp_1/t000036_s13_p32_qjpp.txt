The lack of consideration of abortion as an essential service
within a comprehensive package of sexual and reproductive health services
has a negative impact on rural women. In several countries
where abortion policies are considered liberal, access is constrained by
the requirement that the procedure is performed by specialists to
the exclusion of mid-level providers who are more likely to
be available ( 34 ). The lack of trained specialists
in rural health facilities means that most women cannot obtain
abortion within the 10-week gestational limit ( 58 ). This
limitation pushes rural women back to the routes of unsafe
abortions, which puts their risk of death at 208 deaths
per 100,000 procedures. This figure is four times higher than
if the abortion is performed in medical clinics ( 34
).
