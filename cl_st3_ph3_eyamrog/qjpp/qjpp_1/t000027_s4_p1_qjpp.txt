In 2013, WHO Director-General Margaret Chan warned that “[e]fforts to
prevent noncommunicable diseases go against the business interests of powerful
economic operators. In my view, this is one of the
biggest challenges facing health promotion” ( 19 ). How we
understand the nature of CDoH will inform priority action to
reduce their harmful influences. The initial focus on a small
number of products and their production by large TNCs in
selected industries has shaped the current boundaries of public health
action. From the late 1990s, the tobacco control community focused
much needed attention on industry products and practices that globally
now kill more than 8 million people annually, including 1.2
million people exposed to secondhand smoke. The public health community
then extended these insights to other industries that produce health-harming
products, including alcohol, food and beverage, and pharmaceuticals. The health
harms from oil and gas, mining, firearms, gambling, fashion, digital
technology, and other industries have now come under scrutiny.
