Over the past 30 years, the term social epidemiology has
been increasingly used to refer to a subfield within epidemiology
that focuses on understanding how social organization shapes the distribution
of health and disease. Social organization encompasses a range of
structures and systems, including social and economic relationships, rules and
policies, and norms. It includes a broad set of factors
such as social structure and levels of inequality, the economy,
distribution of resources and organization of work, social hierarchies and
relations between racial and ethnic groups, as well as cultural
factors and social norms. In contrast with other areas within
epidemiology that are defined by the health outcome, social epidemiology
is defined by its focus on the broad set of
social conditions that affect many health-related outcomes ( 59 ,
67 ).
