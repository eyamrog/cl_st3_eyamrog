Although an understanding of the SECPC is a key principle
in IS ( 96 ), “theories, models and frameworks used
in implementation science” ( 90 ) had not directly taken
on health equity. More recently, IS has embraced the call
to advance health equity. Several authors provide guidance for centering
health equity in IS ( 8 , 14 , 22
, 39 , 80 , 111 , 112 ). Some
of these authors recognize that achieving health equity goals will
require IS researchers to sit with discomfort in applying methods
that are less reductionist and embrace the messiness and complexity
of health equity models that center the historical, economic, political,
and social forces that impact well-being ( 8 , 14
). Yet, none of these IS health equity guidance documents
or frameworks specifically call out rural disparities or apply an
intersectional lens.
