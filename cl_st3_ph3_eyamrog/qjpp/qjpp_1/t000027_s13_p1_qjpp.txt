Approaches to mitigate the harmful health effects of CDoH have
generally targeted either the consumer (demand side) or producer (supply
side) of those harms. Demand-side approaches, centered on behavioral change,
have been most widely used. However, evidence of the substantial
and varied influences of commercial actors on consumption behaviors has
spurred attention to supply-side strategies. Accumulating research on the strategies
of relevant industries, revealed through litigation, whistleblowers, and internal industry
documents, has also supported this shift ( 12 , 131
). Limited attention to date has been given to the
structural factors that shape the social conditions within which consumers
and producers of CDoH come together.
