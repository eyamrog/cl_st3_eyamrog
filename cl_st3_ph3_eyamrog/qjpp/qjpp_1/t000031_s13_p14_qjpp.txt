Racial disparities in prediabetes prevalence mirror those seen for diabetes.
In the United States, there is a higher prevalence of
prediabetes among non-Hispanic blacks and Hispanics than among non-Hispanic Asian
participants ( 69 ). Although the prevalence of prediabetes among
Asians is lower than that of their white counterparts, they
had a substantially lower BMI ( 69 ). The higher
prevalence of prediabetes and higher risk of cardiometabolic outcomes at
BMI cut points in Asians and Asian Americans is a
source of controversy; thus, recent recommendations advocate using lower BMI
cut points in this population ( 48 ).
