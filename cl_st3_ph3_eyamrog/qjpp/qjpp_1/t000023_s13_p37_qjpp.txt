Most importantly, the questions posed in social epidemiology, the data
collected, the types of analyses done, and the way in
which results are interpreted are all shaped in part by
social and historical contexts and by the experiences and backgrounds
of the researchers themselves. For these reasons, ensuring that researchers
in social epidemiology reflect diverse backgrounds and lived experiences (in
addition to different disciplines) is fundamental to the scientific rigor
of the field and the social utility of the work
it produces.
