Another goal of reproducible research is to provide a kind
of audit trail, should one be needed. In fact, one
could suggest a definition of reproducible research as “research that
can be checked.” Desiring an audit trail for data analyses
raises the question of when such an audit trail might
be used. In general, one might be interested in seeing
the details of the data and the code for an
analysis when a researcher is curious about how a specific
result was reached. Sometimes that curiosity is raised because of
suspicion of an error in the analysis, but other times
there is a desire to learn the details of new
techniques or methodologies ( 21 ). Thus, reproducibility concerns primarily
the integrity and transparency of the data analysis for an
investigation. Unlike replication, reproducibility allows for an internal check on
the results and is not immediately connected to the context
of the outside world.
