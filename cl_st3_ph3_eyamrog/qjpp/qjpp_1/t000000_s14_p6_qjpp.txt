Parameters for national control measures and death rates were determined
as maximum likelihood estimates, using data estimates for the total
number of all new infections and deaths (together with levels
of uncertainty) as proposed by the Institute for Health Metrics
and Evaluation (IHME) . Due to inconsistencies and under-reporting of
COVID-19 metrics in many countries, rather than relying on official
reports, their estimates are made based on excess mortality statistics,
comparing death rates in each country during the pandemic to
historical data, tracking past trends and seasonality. Other similar excess
mortality estimates have been made elsewhere with reasonable consistency; for
instance, Karlinsky et al. estimated 160,000 deaths in South Africa
by 27 June 2021 (60,000 reported) compared to the IHME
estimate of 156,373 deaths (with a high and low estimate
range of 90,221 to 258,352).
