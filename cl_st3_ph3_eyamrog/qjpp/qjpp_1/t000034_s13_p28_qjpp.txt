We see cash transfers broadly as social policy and interventions
that provide material means for basic social and economic conditions.
These measures affect inequality in society and thus have a
direct bearing on the extent to which people from different
social circumstances have access to health-related resources. However, the health
implications of these policies are rarely discussed. To enable assessment
of their effects on health inequalities, we recommend producing research-based
health-impact statements for such policies ( 75 ).
