At its core, social epidemiology is about generating fundamental knowledge.
Because of its object of study, social epidemiology must necessarily
be multimethod, interdisciplinary, and broad. Sometimes the knowledge generated by
social epidemiology will lead to specific prescriptions for action, but
other times (perhaps even most times) it will not. Rather,
it will provide input into the broader social and political
debate about the distribution and drivers of ill-health and their
implications. This debate is what will ultimately drive actions and
social change.
