Taken together, these experiments reveal that normal FUS function is
essential for perinatal mouse survival and that P517L and, to
a lesser extent, Δ14 mutant FUS protein retain sufficient activity
for a single copy of either allele to rescue the
knockout phenotype, albeit partially. However, when expressed at higher levels
in homozygous or compound heterozygous animals, mutant FUS protein is
toxic in a dose-dependent manner, decreasing mutant survival. This toxicity
is associated with increased insolubility of FUS, TDP-43 and related
RBPs, many of which have been independently implicated in the
pathogenesis of ALS. This insolubility leads to the consequent loss
of function of these regulatory proteins and to the aberrant
processing of their respective target RNAs.
