When re-analyzing the sequence data, we relied on the mutation
frequencies reported in each publication, notably because raw data were
unavailable in most of the papers. Although the methods used
for mapping and base-calling somewhat differed among the different papers
(Supplementary Table 7 ), in line with the above, we
focused on mutations with a frequency higher than 80%, which
was not present in the first timepoint sequenced. This led
to a conservative approach that is likely less affected by
the differences in the various publications’ bioinformatics approaches. When analyzing
viral polymorphisms across time (Fig. 3 ), we also present
mutations at lower frequencies, as reported by each individual publication,
for illustrative purposes. All relevant clinical data were collected and
are summarized in Table 1 and Supplementary Tables 2 and
3 . Ct values for RT–qPCR tests were also extracted
from all publications (Supplementary Table 6 ). A test was
considered borderline if the Ct ranged from 34 to 37;
a test was considered negative if it was reported as
such or was equal to 38 or higher. In one
study , raw VL measurements were presented in terms of
number of copies per milliliter of sample, and a negative
result was reported when zero copies were detected.
