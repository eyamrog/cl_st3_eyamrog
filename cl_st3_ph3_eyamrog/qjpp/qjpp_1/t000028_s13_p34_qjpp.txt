Poverty, racial and ethnic discrimination, and structural oppression based on
race, ethnicity, gender, citizenship status, and class are root causes
of health disparities and diet-related diseases, yet urban food policy
making has often ignored these social determinants. Actions to address
root causes of food-related problems such as poverty or residential
segregation are often addressed by distinct bureaucracies, such as economic
development and housing agencies, that are neither coordinated nor charged
with linking their policies to food access, food insecurity, and
nutrition ( 32 , 66 ).
