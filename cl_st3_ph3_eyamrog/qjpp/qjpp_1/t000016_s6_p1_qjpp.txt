AI has shown promise for diagnosis and grading of prostate
cancer, but these results have been restricted to siloed studies
with limited proof for generalization across diverse multinational cohorts, representing
one of the central barriers to implementation of AI algorithms
in clinical practice. The objective of the present study was
to overcome these critical issues. First, we aimed to facilitate
community-driven development of AI algorithms for cancer detection and grading
on prostate biopsies. Second, we sought to transcend isolated assessment
of the diagnostic performance of individual AI solutions by focusing
on reproducibility and fully blinded validation of a diverse group
of algorithms on intercontinental and multinational cohorts.
