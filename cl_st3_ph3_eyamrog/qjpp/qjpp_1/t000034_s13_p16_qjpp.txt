In social welfare and economic development policy and research, cash
transfers are often defined narrowly as cash assistance, also known
as means-tested transfers and/or social assistance, wherein money is given
to a targeted vulnerable population. In the United States, such
policy examples include Temporary Assistance for Needy Families, the Earned
Income Tax Credit (EITC; delivered as a refundable tax benefit—in
effect, a negative income tax—to low-wage workers and their families),
and the Supplemental Nutritional Assistance Program, among others. Temporary Assistance
for Needy Families has shrunk and no longer accounts for
a large share of safety-net spending ( 34 ). In
2019, about $361 billion, or 8% of the federal budget,
went to safety-net programs ( 23 ).
