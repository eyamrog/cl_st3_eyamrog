Publications in the academic and practice literature use the above
definitions and/or create their own definitions. Many of these other
definitions of rural also depend on their relative comparison to
urban, suggesting that rural is valid only from an urban
viewpoint ( 89 , chapter 5). For example, urban areas
are often defined precisely, whereas rural areas are all those
not urban or with less population or at some distance
from urban areas ( 10 ). At the same time,
people's conception of urban is diverse, whereas their visual image
of rural is singular, often white men and farmers (
10 ). Some definitions are also developed from an economic
perspective (e.g., commuting time for employment) and thus are insufficient
for a broader understanding of well-being ( 33 ).
