A number of randomized, controlled trials have been conducted to
evaluate the benefits of various pharmaceutical interventions for diabetes prevention
( Table 3 ). These trials have shown diabetes risk
reductions ranging from 25% to 70% as compared with placebo,
depending on the drug used and the duration of follow-up.
Metformin was assessed in the DPP trial (31% diabetes risk
reduction versus placebo over 3 years) ( 56 ) and
the Indian DPP (26.4% risk reduction versus placebo over 2.5
years) ( 86 ). Thiazolidinediones were investigated in the DPP
trial (75% by troglitazone over 1 year versus placebo) (
26 ), the DREAM trial (62% reduction by rosiglitazone over
3 years versus placebo) ( 30 ), and the ACTOS
Now trial (72% reduction by pioglitazone over 3 years versus
placebo) ( 23 ). Alpha glucosidase inhibitors were assessed in
the STOP-NIDDM trial (25% risk reduction by acarbose over 3
years versus placebo) ( 18 ) and in a Japanese
study (40% risk reduction by voglibose over 1 year versus
placebo) ( 54 ). Glucagon-like peptide-1 analogues also have a
significant effect on diabetes incidence (79% risk reduction by liraglutide
over 3 years versus placebo) ( 61 , 84 ).
