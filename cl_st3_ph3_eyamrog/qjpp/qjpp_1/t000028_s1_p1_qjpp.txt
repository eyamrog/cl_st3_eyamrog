Over the past several decades, cities worldwide have attempted to
reconfigure their food systems to improve public health, advance social
justice, and promote environmental resilience using diverse municipal policies, often
with the support of stakeholder-led governance mechanisms such as food
policy councils. This article reviews the roles that cities have
played in creating healthful urban food systems and the effects
of those policies on public health. It explains that despite
wide-ranging policy initiatives, disparities in food insecurity and malnourishment persist.
It concludes by describing several promising pathways for urban food
policy: engaging in food-focused urban planning to create equitable food
environments; treating policies to address inequality and social justice as
upstream food policies; considering the effects of new business models
such as online food retail in urban food policy making;
and using food procurement as a lever to influence regional,
national, and global food systems.
