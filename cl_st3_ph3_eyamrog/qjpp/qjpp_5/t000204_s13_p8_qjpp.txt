NAGPRA's underpinnings are legal in nature, and a number of
scholars have sought to frame NAGPRA's requirements within archaeological constructs
or Native American ideas of kinship and social identity (
Dongoske et al. 1997 , Ferguson 2004 , Liebmann 2008
, Welch & Ferguson 2007 ). Liebmann (2008) criticizes the
essentialism inherent in NAGPRA, which, among other things, restricts claimants
to include only members of federally recognized tribes and lineal
descendants. The former are bureaucratic constructs of a colonizing government;
