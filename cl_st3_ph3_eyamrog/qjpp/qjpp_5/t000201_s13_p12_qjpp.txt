The characteristic settlement type of the Late Neolithic and Copper
Age of Iberia is the walled hilltop settlement, made up
of thick walls and circular bastions. They are best known
along the river valleys of west-central Portugal and southeast Spain.
Their defensive qualities have long been explained by exogenous influences.
Siret, for example, argued that these sites, such as Los
Millares, were Phoenician trading posts ( Siret 1913 , p.
39). Do Paço & Jalhay (1942) , who excavated Vila
Nova de São Pedro between 1937 and 1967, discerned similarities
between Vila Nova and other sites in southeast Iberia (such
as Los Millares) and, like Siret, were sympathetic to the
idea that Vila Nova was inspired by cultures and colonists
from the Mediterranean Levant.
