In a world where men seemed the outliers in most
ritual practice after the demographic devastations of Stalinist purges and
World War II, only one woman was publicly regarded in
the small rural setting for her willingness to help those
in need. She buttonholed me one day on the street,
seemingly after some drink, to ask why I had never
come by to see her and insisting that, yes, she
could tell me things. I showed up the next afternoon
at her flat in one of the small town's multistory
buildings, off a muddy street that I did not know
well, to find that she had given me the slip.
Her daughter insisted that she had gone off without notice.
Were there any shamans in this closed military zone of
the Soviet Union's far eastern outpost in the twilight of
socialism? Not least when Siberia has been long held to
be the earliest recorded hearth of shamanic experience? Possibly? Probably.
It's hard to tell.
