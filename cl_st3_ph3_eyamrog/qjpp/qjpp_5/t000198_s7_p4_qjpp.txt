In this review, I explore this multifaceted subdiscipline beginning with
its origins and concluding with the present. Therefore, I (
a ) survey the literature on primatomorphic depictions and zooarchaeological
remains of haplorhines and strepsirhines , ( b ) analyze
recurrent topics that characterize archaeoprimatological studies, ( c ) examine
patterns of universals and particularities of the archaeoprimatological evidence in
the zooarchaeological record of different regions of the world, (
d ) present elements of current ethnoprimatological investigations that can
be used to understand the past interface of all members
of the order Primates , and ( e ) propose
ideas about future lines of archaeoprimatological research.
