The frequency of predation events by predators on capuchins is
unknown. Although direct observations of predation do occur, they are
rare. For example, Tórrez et al. (2012) reported a lethal
attack by a jaguar on a capuchin on BCI. However,
capuchins have been confirmed to be part of the diet
of jaguars, pumas, and ocelots via analysis of fecal contents
( Chinchilla, 1997 , Moreno and Giacalone, 2006 ). The
absence of all these mammalian predators in Coiba National Park
creates an opportunity to investigate if capuchins use the terrestrial
niche more extensively when predation risk is reduced.
