How patient is any among us, Benjamin and Pannwitz ask,
to remain open to radical difference? A look back at
the earlier anthropological record indicates that structural patience, at least,
was often in short supply, when shamans have been cast
for generations as decidedly male, all-knowing, gallantly traveling, and doing
good on behalf of others. Moving away from these models
takes a leap of faith that has nothing to do
with the supernatural: conceding a mastery over knowing and finding
inside ourselves worlds that we may never entirely comprehend.
