Portmann observed that most precocial species have newborns that are
“miniature versions of their parents” but that such a condition
is not the case for human newborns. He wrote that
the body proportions of other species are similar in infants
and adults, whereas in humans those ratios are quite different.
In a comparison of human and chimpanzee body proportions, Portmann
showed that chimpanzees at birth were more similar to adults
in their proportions than are humans whose legs are longer
relative to body size in adults than they are in
infants ( Portmann 1990 , Trevathan & Rosenberg 2016b ).
