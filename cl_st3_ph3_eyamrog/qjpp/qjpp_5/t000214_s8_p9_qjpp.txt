Many life history events include disruptions to the individual's social
environment, such as rejection by its mother during weaning, the
onset of reproductive behaviors, changes in social status and novel
relationships. In such situations, the HPA axis is activated, and
hormones such as glucocorticoids are released into the bloodstream, enabling
the individual to cope ( Sapolsky, 1987 , 2000 ).
Due to this physiological disruption and hormonal response, important life
history events in primates may also correlate with the formation
of accentuated lines ( Dirks et al., 2002 , 2010
). This relationship is well established for one life history
event: the neonatal line forms in teeth at birth (
Schour, 1936 ; Norén, 1984 ; Eli et al., 1989
; Zanolli et al., 2011 ; Witzel, 2014 ; Kurek
et al., 2016 ).
