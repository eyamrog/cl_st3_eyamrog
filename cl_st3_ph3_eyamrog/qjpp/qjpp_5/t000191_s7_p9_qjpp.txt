One answer, I suggest, comes in a history of social
relations where shamans are cast as translators of the unseen
and are themselves sites of anxiety in a very real
world, a world filled with encounters across lines of gender,
class, and colonial incursions often defined by race. In Wayward
Shamans: The Prehistory of an Idea (2013), Tomášková joins earlier
work on gender in shamanism ( Bacigalupo 2007 ; Boddy
1994 ; Buyandelger 2013 ; Humphrey & Onon 1996 ;
Kendall 1985 , 1988 ; Van Deusen 2001 , 2004
) by expertly mapping how a sea of evidence showcasing
female spirit leaders in the archaeological record was set aside
in favor of patriarchal archetypes in a long history of
gender panic.
