The twenty-first century beckons a new era for Asian paleoanthropology.
New data are accumulating at a fast rate, and old
data are becoming easily accessible with digital archiving. What used
to be a dearth of fossil hominins is now being
replaced with an ever-increasing fossil sample that will be better
dated, and with a magnitude and pattern of variation that
exceed previous understanding. Renewed understandings of old data welcome new
perspectives that ask us to reflect and project. With these
trends in place, Asia may find itself at the center
of investigations crucial to understanding human evolution throughout the Pleistocene.
The past decade has revealed that the Asian hominin fossil
record includes a spectrum of populations from the caves of
Siberia to the forests of Java, showcasing an interesting focal
point in the story of human evolution.
