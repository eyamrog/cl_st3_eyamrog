The divergence times between closely related genomes from either side
of Wallace's Line provide estimates for the timing of possible
eastward dispersal events of M. fascicularis : (i) the most
recent common ancestor of subclade B1-a1 is estimated to have
lived 30 ka ago (95% CI = 40–20 ka); (ii)
within clade B2, subclade B2-a includes two sequences from Penida,
which is west of Wallace's Line, and from Lombok, which
is east; the most recent common ancestor of this clade
is 60 ka (90–40 ka); and (iii) within clade B3,
subclade B3-a includes four sequences from Bali and one from
Lombok; the most recent common ancestor of subclade B3-a is
10 ka (20–0 ka). Dispersal scenarios associated with these divergence
times and also the divergence between subclades B1-a, and B1-b
( Fig. 3 ) are presented in the Discussion section.
