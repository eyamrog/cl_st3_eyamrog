These indigenist/gradualist models involving local agency find some support in
the archaeological record. For example, at Vale Pincel I, in
coastal Portugal, in a level dated to 5500 cal BCE,
microliths of the local Mesolithic tradition were found associated with
Neolithic objects, including impressed ceramics and polished stone axes (
Soares & Tavares da Silva 2003 ). At other sites
in coastal Portugal, Neolithic levels with domestic animals and Cardial
ceramics are found with wild animals, such as boar (
Sus scrofa ) and red deer ( Cervus elaphus )
( Carvalho 2008 ). Craniometric studies of Mesolithic and Early
Neolithic samples from central and southern Portugal, which show demographic
continuity over time, have also been used to support an
indigenist or gradualist model ( Jackes et al. 1997 ).
