“Luna Cynocephalo visum exhilaratģ, adimitģ, sunt & conjugibus mutua fata
bonis” (the moon delightfully seen by the dog-headed, they are
a couple for the mutual good fate) appeared in 1595
in a book written by the German erudite Joachim Camerarius
(1595 , p. 84) (1500–1574). A xylography of a baboon
worshipping the moon accompanied the text ( Figure 1 a
). This was a primary image that presented an original
example of the translatability of an adored baboon from ancient
Egypt to Renaissance Europe through the lens of European scholars.
