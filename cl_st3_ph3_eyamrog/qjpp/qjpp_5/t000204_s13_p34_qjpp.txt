NAGPRA has been the law of the land in the
United States for three decades. Although other countries may not
yet have similar laws formally on the books, the repatriation
movement has gained steam during that time period. Atalay recently
declared that “repatriation is healing” ( Shannon et al. 2017
, p. 89). Whether or not one agrees with that
sentiment, repatriation has stimulated and brought about fundamental changes in
anthropology, archaeology, and museum studies, not to mention tribal cultural
and religious practices, and it will continue to do so
in the future. NAGPRA tends to alter everyone who encounters
it ( Bradley 2018 , Preucel 2011 ).
