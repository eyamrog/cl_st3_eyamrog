Connecting back to our principles above, these interactions involve trade-offs
between branches of immunity as well as the development of
tolerance in both the immunological and ecological senses. Evolved dependencies
are likely to evolve as a form of tolerance because
tolerance often involves shifts in allocations to compensate for the
presence of the pathogen. For example, increases in Treg cells
reduce resistance to a helminth infection but may also help
limit immunopathology from failed attempts at clearance. Changes in IgE
and natural killer cell counts may help restrain a helminth
infection without killing it. These changes may facilitate parasite survival
but also facilitate host tolerance.
