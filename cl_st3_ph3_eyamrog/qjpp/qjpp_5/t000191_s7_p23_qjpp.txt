In these lights, Buyandelger (2013) helps us to identify shamanism
as “modernity's disowned creation” (p. 28), akin to what Tomášková
(2013) calls the paradoxical “agnatology” at the heart of shamanic
scholarship, “a cultural and historically induced willingness not to know”
and “a stubborn desire not to find out” about shamanism's
direct provenance from the colonial encounter (p. 53). “Shaman,” instead,
rapidly became “a word in motion” ( Gluck & Tsing
2009 ) with global traction.
