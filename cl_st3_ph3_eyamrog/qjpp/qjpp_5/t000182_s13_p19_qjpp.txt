Breaches can be the origin point of the social relation.
Somatic splits, whether incomplete or fully severing, ground case studies
of amputation and prosthesis ( Nelson 2001 ). In many
ways, breaches resonate with broader anthropological theories of the partiality
of persons, such that rupturing the intactness of personhood constitutes
exchange and relationality ( Strathern 2020 ). Medical anthropology often
engages with and reconfigures this theme of partiality, sometimes more
biopolitically and sometimes more biomorally, as evidenced in ethnographies of
organ transplants and the traffic in human tissues ( Cohen
1999 , 2011 ; Copeman & Banerjee 2019 ; Crowley-Matoka
2016 ; Hamdy 2012 ; Sanal 2011 ; Sharp 2006
; Waldby & Mitchell 2006 ). A body may be
breached so that its parts can be given over to
another, a relational ethic that Cohen (2013) terms “excorporation as
commitment.”
