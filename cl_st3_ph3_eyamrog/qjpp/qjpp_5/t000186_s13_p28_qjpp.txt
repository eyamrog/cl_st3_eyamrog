The presence of critical periods and transgenerational effects means that
the effects of parasite or microbe exposure will not be
the same for everyone. For example, when individuals who were
never exposed to parasites in childhood are infected with helminths,
they may mount a strong resistant response, possibly resulting in
an immunological outcome opposite of that which was desired. This
finding may help explain why clinical trials for helminth therapy
have proven equivocal, despite robust associations with helminth infections in
population studies ( Helmby 2015 ). Similar effects may also
complicate microbiome restoration effects, for example following C-section delivery (
Wilson et al. 2021 ).
