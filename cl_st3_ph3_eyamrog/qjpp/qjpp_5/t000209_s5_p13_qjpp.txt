Similar to M. fascicularis , the deepest diversification within silenus
group macaques is found in the Sunda region. Because we
did not sequence a mitochondrial genome of a M. nemestrina
specimen from south Sumatra, we were unable to determine whether
this early bifurcation occurs in mitochondrial DNA of M. nemestrina
from northern and southern Sumatra, which is the case with
M. fascicularis ( Figure 2 , Figure 3 ; Yao
et al., 2017 ), or from Sumatra and Borneo. On
Sulawesi, mitochondrial diversity generally clusters by species, but with poorly
resolved relationships among some of these clusters. This phylogenetic pattern
with short internodes and long terminal branches matches expectations for
an initially expanding population ( Nordborg, 2001 ). Monophyly of
mitochondrial genomes (outside of hybrid zones) is consistent with the
proposal that evolution of much of Sulawesi's terrestrial fauna was
influenced by habitat fragmentation ( Evans et al., 2003b ).
