“Wound,” “wounded,” and “wounding” as key terms for object, status,
and action are both somatic and clinical terms that refer
to bodies in and out of the domains of medicine.
The Oxford English Dictionary (2021) defines a wound as “a
hurt caused by the laceration or separation of the tissues
of the body.” An equally fleshy definition derives from surgery,
which tends to define wounds in terms of their relative
degree of trauma, with “trauma” meaning blunt or penetrating injuries
that are immediately life-threatening. “Wounding,” in turn, is the causative
act of creating injury. "Wounded” works as an adjective that
describes being inflicted with injury, and the collective noun form
“the wounded” expresses a polity of the injured.
