As suggested by Urbani (2013) , archaeoprimatological research explores the
shared nexus between humans and primates in time and space
to examine the ways such bonds persist, vary, change, or
end. As a result, humans and primates impact each other
and likely determine their own existences. From past to present,
humans have culturally constructed primates and primates have depended on
humans for their survival. Thus, advancing archaeoprimatology, as is the
case for ethnoprimatology ( Fuentes 2012 , Leblan 2013 ,
Riley 2018 ; and Dore et al. 2017 , Urbani
& Lizarralde 2020 , and chapters therein), will require multiple
ontologies, critical views, and various intellectual perspectives. It should be
based on solid descriptive, analytical, and integrative approaches. This path
will be fecund as long as novel techniques are used
for and new questions are asked about the longue durée
interface between humans and primates (see Future Issues).
