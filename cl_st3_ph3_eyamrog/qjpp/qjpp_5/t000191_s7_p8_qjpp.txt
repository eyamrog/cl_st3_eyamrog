My lead question in this review is, therefore, if our
knowledge of shamanism has been so significantly selective [ Eliade
1964 (1951)], so surprisingly uneven ( Tomášková 2013 ), so
deeply varied across time and space ( Atkinson 1989 ,
Bacigalupo 2016 , Flaherty 1992 , Thomas & Humphrey 1994
, Taussig 1987 ), and so enduringly skeptical ( Lévi-Strauss
1963a , b ) for so long, how has its
study come to occupy such pride of place in the
anthropological canon?
