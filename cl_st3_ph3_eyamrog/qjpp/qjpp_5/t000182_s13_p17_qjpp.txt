Consider some alternative pathways in these thought experiments. What if
disability had been the first proper attachment of medical anthropology,
and disease had to make a case for itself decades
later? These hypotheticals are just that, of course, and they
are also rhetorical generalizations that obscure the fact that disability
scholars have long insisted on a more capacious sense of
medical anthropological study. Still, hypotheticals can reveal certain intensities of
ethnographic attachments that have marginalized other analyses of body politics
and the body politic.
