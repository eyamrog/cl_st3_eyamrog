Additionally, an often-cited hypothesis is the so-called ‘fighting hypothesis’ (
Faurie and Raymond, 2013 ) to explain the strong population
bias in handedness. It proposes that left-handedness is associated with
fitness-decreasing traits—such as higher vulnerability for several diseases—but is maintained
in the population by negative frequency-dependent selection on co-occurring fitness
benefits. For the latter, they propose a higher chance of
winning fights due to a surprise effect. However, this hypothesis
has received criticism. Direct evidence for the negative frequency-dependent selection
process on winning fights has not yet been published, and
other evidence in favor of the hypothesis has been challenged
(for a review, see Groothuis et al., 2013 ).
