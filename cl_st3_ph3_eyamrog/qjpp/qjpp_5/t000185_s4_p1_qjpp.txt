Reorienting scholarship toward the biolegal offers a lens through which
we can study how the biosciences and their attendant technologies
are reconfiguring core anthropological concepts such as exchange, self, kinship,
and community. Ultimately, our aim is not merely to reframe
this scholarship under the banner of biolegality, but also to
make the point that coordinated attention from anthropology can redefine
the epistemological and normative contours of debates around law and
biotechnology. Biolegality, then, has an important function in society and
merits a valued place next to the study of biopolitics,
bioethics, or biolaw.
