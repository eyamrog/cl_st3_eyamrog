In assessing sociocultural anthropology during 2020, Berry suggests that anthropology
might work as “a procession for collective dis-ease,” meaning an
anthropology guided by “the felt presence of irremediable loss, which
is to feel more deeply driven by that which makes
premature death an immanent and ever-present reality in ‘normal times’”
( Berry 2021 , p. 942). Taking seriously Berry's call
to reckon and forge an anthropology that feels loss, the
review questions the place of wounds in relation to that
feeling, amid an ongoing scaling up of bodies to politics.
