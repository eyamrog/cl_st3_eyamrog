Although the index body of wound culture is often assumed
to be that of the injured person, it can also
be that of the unwounded, spectating person who desires to
watch violence in the bodies of others. This spectator-oriented theory
underlies Seltzer's (1997) definition of wound culture. For Seltzer, wound
culture involves the American cultural obsession with scenes of murder
and crime ( Seltzer 2013 , 2018 ). His notion
of wound culture also rests on the mediation of those
scenes through film, television, and news. For Seltzer, the ur
-object of wound culture in the United States is the
television medical drama ER, a public cultural form he casts
as “pure wound culture—the world, half meat and half machinery
in a perpetual state of emergency” ( Seltzer 2013 ,
p. 26).
