In Taï and LuiKotale, feeding competition, intragroup infanticide, and intergroup
violence may be less severe threats than in Budongo. However,
these two populations, and the mothers within them, face predation
pressures that have become largely absent in most other Pan
populations ( Boesch, 1991 ; D'Amour et al., 2006 ;
Wood et al., 2017 ), including in Budongo. Therefore, the
comparatively stable levels of gregariousness demonstrated by Taï and LuiKotale
mothers may manifest not only from benign low levels of
competition and violence but also as larger groups offer better
protection against predation. In Budongo and other populations where Pan
predators are absent, mothers may have more flexibility in rearing
strategies and association patterns, which is additionally advantageous if population
densities and resource availability heighten competition.
