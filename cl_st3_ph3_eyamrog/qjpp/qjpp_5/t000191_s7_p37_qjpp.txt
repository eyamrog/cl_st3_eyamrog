This pull between the horizontal and vertical could, at times,
even shift encounters between communist leaders and “wild” Darhads, where
the former needed the latter to make their rule legible,
effectively reprising the earlier role of Buddhist monks, a “weighty
center,” challenging the relatively “weightless (shamanic) margin” ( Pedersen 2011
, p. 146). In this analysis, he writes, “Shamanism is
not so much a symbolic projection of one type of
content (‘politics’ or ‘economics’) onto another type of content (‘religion’).…[Instead]
shamanism is the (impossible) form assumed by the world in
northern Mongolia after socialism. Shamanism is, so to speak, an
ontology of transition” (p. 35). As in the Amazon, he
adds, it all comes down to the form that beings
assume and the shapes of the spirit world they join.
