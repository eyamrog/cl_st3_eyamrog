Of 48 entries of locations with allochthonous primates ( Supplemental
Table 2 ), only 24 provided information on the ages
of the primates. From this subsample, 79% (19/24) of sites
have young individuals (infant, juveniles, and subadults), and the remaining
sites have adults (13%) or both adults and young animals
(Saqqara and Tuna el-Gebel: 8%). When the reported role of
the allochthonous primates was determined, it appears that primates were
kept as pets (56%, or 27/48, of sites), served in
ritual contexts (10%), and functioned as both pets and sacred
animals (23%). Primates were consumed as food at the remaining
sites (10%). This evidence indicates that to ensure the survival
of allochthonous monkeys, or to have primates that will serve
longer as animal companions, they were selected, transported, and kept
as young animals. This information confirms that primates were valuable
commodities and were traded from distant lands for leisure and
symbolic functions.
