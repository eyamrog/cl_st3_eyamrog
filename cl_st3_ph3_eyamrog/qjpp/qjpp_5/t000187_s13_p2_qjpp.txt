The incorporation of Indigenous peoples into settler-colonial society is facilitated
through the dissolution of Native land title into alienable individual
freeholdings, the erosion of tribal political sovereignty, religious conversion, and
coerced assimilation. To fill the demographic void produced by Indigenous
elimination and the economic demand for labor created by the
territorial expansion of the colonial state, workers are imported into
the colony. In the United States, this new labor force
was initially made up of African slaves but was subsequently
filled by new waves of European, Black, and brown immigrants
( Wolfe 1999 , p. 3). The concept of race
serves as the organizing grammar of the settler-colonial state and
has been used to systematically justify the hegemony of white
European settlers, to police and erase Indigenous claims to land,
and to legitimize the enslavement of Black labor.
