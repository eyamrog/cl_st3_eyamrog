In the history of modern paleoanthropology, Asia occupied a marginal
space in the second half of the twentieth century, secondary
to the attention given to Europe and Africa. Europe was
the place for modern human origins debate, with a focus
on the evolutionary history of the Neanderthals and their relationship
with modern humans. Africa was the place for hominin origins,
the only continent where early hominins were discovered. In comparison,
Asia did not receive much attention in either of these
topics. The attention it did receive was shaped by Eurocentrism
and colonialism ( Athreya & Ackermann 2020 ).
