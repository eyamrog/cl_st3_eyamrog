Reflections on NAGPRA show that many archaeologists, particularly in academia,
consider it complicated, difficult, burdensome, or even worse ( Meighan
1992 ). Yet, for the most part, NAGPRA has empowered
some tribes to reclaim their heritage and pushed museums to
be more oriented toward Native concerns and critiques ( Chari
& Lavallee 2013 , Graham & Murphy 2010 , Lippert
2015 , Wilcox 2010 ). It has led museums to
acknowledge the “appalling fact” that not all museum collections were
properly cataloged (see Isaac 2002 , p. 164). In fact,
archaeologists recognized this so-called “curation crisis” in print as early
as 1982, nearly a decade before the passage of NAGPRA
( Marquardt et al. 1982 ), but their focus was
on archaeological collections. NAGPRA applies to archaeological, ethnographic, art, and
some biological collections as well.
