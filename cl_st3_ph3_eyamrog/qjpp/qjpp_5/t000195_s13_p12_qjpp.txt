For Tobias, this similarity of features in some Makapansgat fossils
to those of A. ( P. ) robustus supported a
notion that Paranthropus was an invalid genus, and he disagreed
with Robinson (1962) , who maintained that Paranthropus showed dietary
specializations in its dentition and cranial structure that rendered it
distinct generically from Australopithecus . As with Dart and Broom
before him, Tobias apparently did not entertain the possibility that
pronounced morphological variation in the samples from Sterkfontein and Makapansgat
could indicate that two distinct species were both present within
each of the two sites.
