More recently, scholars studying Indigenous identity formation in settler-colonial contexts
have broadened the concept of ethnogenesis to emphasize cultural persistence
and continuity (see Voss 2015c ). In contrast, a growing
number of African diaspora archaeologists have turned away from diaspora
and toward race and investigations of racial hegemony (e.g., Agbe-Davies
2014 , Barile 2004 , Epperson 2004 , Mullins 1999
, Orser 2001 ). Such critical examinations of racial discourse
within the African diaspora literature have facilitated the formation of
antiracist heritage practices, which are equally applicable to Indigenous contexts.
