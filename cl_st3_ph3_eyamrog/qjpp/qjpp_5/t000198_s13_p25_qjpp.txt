When examining the global uses of primates in areas they
naturally inhabited in the past ( Supplemental Table 2 ),
84% (46/55) of the entries refer to primates as sources
of food. For only five entries (9%) primates had ritualistic
value, and even fewer entries (4%) indicated primates as pets
and sacred animals. Finally, 2% of entries listed primates as
pets and sources of food, and another 2% of entries
referred to primates as sacred animals and sources of food.
Looking closely at the pattern of hunting primates, of 46
entries, only 11 (24%) included data on the age class
of the primate prey. Sixty-four percent (7/11) of this subsample
refers to adult individuals; the remaining are younger (juveniles/subadults). Thus,
in principle, older and likely larger individuals were preferred as
prey.
