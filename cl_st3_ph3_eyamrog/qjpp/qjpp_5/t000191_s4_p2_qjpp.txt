In her reflections on the ontological turn in Amerindian scholarship,
Ramos (2012) lands at a telling moment that speaks to
the isomorphisms between shaman and anthropologist with which I began
this review. Once again, she writes, “The Indians have cosmology,
whereas the Westerners have theory” ( Ramos 2012 , p.
484). “Intellectual efforts notwithstanding, we still find the old ethnographic
division of labor between those who know (the ethnographers) and
those who let themselves be known (the natives)” (p. 490).
Her assessment takes on added resonance when we return to
the Even (s)haman as “the one who knows,” bringing us
back to the heart of the matter. Who, in the
end, has the power to know?
