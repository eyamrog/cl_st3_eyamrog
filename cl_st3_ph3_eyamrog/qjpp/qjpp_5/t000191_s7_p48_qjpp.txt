Back in the Amerindian world, one of the more luminous
recent accounts of shamanic life joins in some of these
sentiments. Shaman Davi Kopenawa is among the Yanomami Indians best
known to the international world through his long-standing public stance
on protecting indigenous rights and lands. Repeatedly citing metamorphosis as
the central physics behind shamanic action, Yanomami ingest yākoana powder
to not simply engage with a world of xapiri beings,
but become them ( Kopenawa & Albert 2013 , p.
277). As his father-in-law and mentor explains to him, spirits
want “to make you become a spirit like them.…This is
why they recognize you and come to you so easily
now! You do not become a ghost without reason!” (p.
76).
