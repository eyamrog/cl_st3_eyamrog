Isomorphism is the process through which organizations come to resemble
one another in terms of practices over time, and generally
occurs in three forms: coercive isomorphism, where organizations adapt in
response to external pressures, especially those from the state; mimetic
isomorphism, where an organization imitates another’s practices to resolve uncertainty;
and normative isomorphism, in which members with similar education and
training join organizations, typically to form professions ( DiMaggio and
Powell 1983 ). Many processes driving isomorphism stem from organizational
embeddedness.
