Data showed that probation officers made implicit assumptions about the
remorse displays of individuals with the characteristics discussed above (violence
“signals”) through two social cognitive processes: fundamental attribution error and
out-group empathy. These social cognitive processes, seemingly triggered by their
interactions with and evaluations of these defendants, appeared to lead
probation officers to doubt defendants’ remorse displays as being disingenuous.
Probation officers were found to exhibit similar remorse bias across
clients with different violent “signals.”
