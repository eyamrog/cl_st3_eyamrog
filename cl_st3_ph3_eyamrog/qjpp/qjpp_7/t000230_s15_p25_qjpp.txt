The previous sections of this paper have identified how the
issue of data itself is problematic in Canada. The availability
of data—current data—to examine issues around education is unlike that
encountered in other comparable democracies. And where it does exist,
it can be tedious to access. I now turn to
the issues a researcher will encounter when undertaking comparative research
with Canadian data, whether it be within-Canada comparative research or
international comparative research. Because much of my research examines the
associations that self-identified race has with education-related outcomes, much of
the focus of my discussion is around the lack of
race data in Canada and its important implications.
