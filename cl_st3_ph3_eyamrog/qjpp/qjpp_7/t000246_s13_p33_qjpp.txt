The history of severe intergroup conflicts substantially affects willingness to
engage in intergroup contact (as well as the trajectory of
actual intergroup encounters). As mentioned before, societies and nations experiencing
wars, genocides, violent colonization, or occupation have a tendency to
engage in narratives centered around their identity as a sole
victim of the conflict (Bilewicz & Liu, 2020 ). Focusing
on one's exclusive victimhood, leads to a decrease in trust,
and makes people more likely to believe in conspiracies and
malevolent plots. This process can ultimately lower contact intentions and
make people distance themselves from outgroup members (Bilewicz & Liu,
2020 ; Bilewicz et al., 2019 ).
