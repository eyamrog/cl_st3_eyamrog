In our review, we show that intimate intergroup contact can
have a range of benefits for majority and minority group
members across the lifespan. We also, however, show that intimate
interactions are not only positive and that effects can vary
depending on the nature and context of the interactions as
well as differ for both majority and minority group members.
Recognizing this complexity, we call on researchers to come together
to develop a deeper understanding of how casual and more
intimate forms of contact might be investigated to determine their
effects (good and bad). Doing so will require innovative methodological
approaches that embrace complexity, move beyond the two-group paradigm and
fully accept the developmental pathways of intergroup contact in applied
as well as experimental settings.
