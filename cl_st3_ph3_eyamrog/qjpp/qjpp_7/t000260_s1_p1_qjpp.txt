The role of socio-economic conditions has been largely implicit in
mathematical epidemiological models. However, measures to address the current pandemic,
specifically the relevant interventions proposing physical distancing, have highlighted how
social determinants affect contagion and mortality dynamics of COVID-19. For
the most part, these social determinants are not present in
either policy discussions or in epidemiological models. We argue for
the importance of incorporating social determinants of health into the
modelling dynamics of COVID-19, and show how global variation of
these conditions may be integrated into relevant models. In doing
so, we also highlight a key political economy aspect of
reproduction dynamics in epidemics.
