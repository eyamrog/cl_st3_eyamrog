The emergence and rapid spread of novel coronavirus disease (COVID-19)
has hit a mostly unprepared world ( Lippi et al.
2020 ). As the emergency has worsened, media attention on
confirmed cases and deaths has increased dramatically, despite the fact
that these data represent the current public health situation with
only limited credibility (see, for example, Lachmann 2020 ). While
most studies in the COVID-19 literature use these (biased) data
to provide more insight into the nature of the virus,
only a few papers investigate the formulation of country reports
1 as a social phenomenon.
