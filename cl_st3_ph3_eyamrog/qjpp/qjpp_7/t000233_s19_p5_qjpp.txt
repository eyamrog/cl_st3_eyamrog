We also probed our motivations for participating in an applied
sociology project. All three of us had personal and professional
reasons for becoming involved, broadly consistent with Uzzi (1996) .
We often identified a personal desire to make direct social
changes which academic work often does not result in. For
example, Jonah wrote, One of the motivations was to feel
like I was giving something to the broader community. My
interest in this was driven by the idea that I
have internalized to some degree that we are “ivory tower”
types who work on their research with very little awareness
or interest in the outside world.
