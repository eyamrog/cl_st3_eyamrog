While some of these motivations clearly stem from pragmatic or
social needs, others are inherently linked to a desire for
personal growth and development through relationships with others. According to
the self-expansion model (Wright et al., 2002 ), contact with
outgroup members offers unique opportunities to acquire new knowledge, perspectives,
and skills. Intergroup contact is sought because it contributes to
satisfying humans’ fundamental need for “expanding” their sense of self
and their resources toward increased self-efficacy. Self-expansion has started to
attract considerable research attention in recent years. Experimental evidence has
confirmed that salient opportunities (Paolini et al., 2020 ) and
personal benefits of self-expansion (Paolini et al., 2016 ) lead
to preferential interest in outgroup members as interaction partners. Moreover,
contact experiences chosen due to high expansion motivation are especially
positive, intimacy-building (Paolini et al., 2006 ) and generate a
sense of self-growth and self-efficacy (Dys-Steenbergen et al., 2016 ).
