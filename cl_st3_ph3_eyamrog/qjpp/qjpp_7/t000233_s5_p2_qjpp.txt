Employees in social services agencies face the task of satisfying
various constituents, often with diverging and even contradictory interests. The
mission of the United Way is to “improve lives and
build community by engaging individuals and mobilizing collective action” (
United Way 2019 ). Looking deeper, the United Way tries
to build a grand consensus of business, organized labor, and
community agencies to address social issues. The United Way touts
its strong links with the local labor movement by listing
dozens of unions as their labor partners besides the hundreds
of businesses that are associated with them ( United Way
2019 ). They are a coalition-type organization. Of course, many
of the businesses on the list of donors belong to
associations which actively campaigned against a rise in the minimum
wage ( Jones 2017 ) and engaged these unions in
contentious collective bargaining. Recognizing these tensions, the United Way has
motivations to maintain a neutral position in labor-management relations.
