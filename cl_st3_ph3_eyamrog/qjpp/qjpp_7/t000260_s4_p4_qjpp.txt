Second, our particular selection of SDs should not be understood
as the only possible relevant factors that could be included
in compartmental models. Even though our preceding analysis has been
largely focused on differences in socio-economic conditions between poorer and
richer countries; the same approach could also be used to
model the impact of social differentiation within individual countries. Issues
such as gender and racial inequalities , educational disparities, levels
of government expenditure on social services, etc. all bear directly
on β and δ ; and could also be incorporated
into a social compartmental model. Furthermore, as heterogeneity in transmission
as a result of SD may also make superspreading events
more likely in particular communities, the study of social inequalities
while incorporating network effects across social groups along the lines
of Chang et al. (2021) and Hébert-Dufresne et al. (2021)
is a particularly interesting research avenue within this theme.
