Knowing more about the nature and consequences of intimate interactions
may have important implications for policy and practice, such as
informing intervention strategies. The pronounced effect of intimate relations on
prejudice as well as health-related variables suggests that in addition
to facilitating mere contact between members of diverse groups, interventions
should be structured to allow for the development of closer
cross-group relationships. Although not all individuals will naturally become close,
repeated opportunities for interaction and the encouragement of reciprocal self-disclosure
may increase the likelihood of this eventuality compared to one-off
encounters and superficial exchanges (White & Abu-Rayya, 2012 ; White
et al., 2014 ). Moreover, the development and advertisement of
friendship between a few group members can have flow-on effects
on other individuals and outgroups (see Boin et al., 2021
).
