Researchers often draw on empathy-altruism theory (EAT) to understand the
motivations behind prosocial behaviors (Batson et al., 2015 ). According
to this theory, prosocial behaviors are evoked by feelings of
empathy towards others. However, feelings may not be a sufficient
antecedent for behavior. According to the theory of planned behavior
(TPB) (Ajzen, 2011 ), behaviors generally result from attitudes, subjective
norms, and perceived behavioral control. The two theories may function
conjunctively and provide a more holistic perspective of individuals’ prosocial
behaviors. Research to date has not attempted to integrate the
two to understand the motives underlying these behaviors. The present
study integrates the two theories to understand beliefs about the
factors that motivate prosocial sentiments and, presumably prosocial behaviors, of
people who were members of the privileged class in Pakistan
during the COVID-19 pandemic. The privileged class in Pakistan consists
of wealthy segments of the society (Robison & Stubager, 2018
) who have socio-economic advantages with readily available financial and
non-financial resources (Albert el al., 2018 ). As members of
the privileged class, they enjoy a variety of psychosocial, economic
and social benefits.
