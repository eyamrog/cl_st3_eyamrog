The goal of this paper is to examine the role
of social factors in the formation of country reports related
to COVID-19 in the early stages of the pandemic. To
this end, a linked database of countries’ COVID-19 reports and
five composite variables (which compress the information content of 162
preliminary selected socioeconomic indicators) is analyzed by using regression models
with regional dummy interactions. The main findings not only reflect
the importance of foreign travel restrictions and airport controls but
also show that in Europe and Africa, more corrupt countries
have reported significantly fewer cases and deaths than countries in
other regions.
