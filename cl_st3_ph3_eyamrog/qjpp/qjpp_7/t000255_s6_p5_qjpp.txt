As subjective memory deteriorated and remained relatively low toward the
beginning of 2021 in this study population, the results of
this study may attest to relatively long-lasting effects of the
pandemic on the subjective memory of middle-aged and older adults.
Future investigations should follow-up on the subjective memory of adults
in later stages of the pandemic to assess whether these
effects shift as the pandemic situation changes. Policymakers should strive
to identify adults with subjective memory declines and work toward
protecting older adults’ cognitive health in the wake of the
pandemic. Such policy measures could include routine screening for memory
declines when older adults come to medical centers and clinics,
as well as implementing social programs to improve cognitive function
(Wesselman et al., 2019 ).
