These findings provide a number of new research questions for
understanding ERS. First, to what extent do parents (and peers)
help children from ethnic-minority backgrounds recognize the potential obstacles for
social mobility? This obstacle is related (but different) from inequities
related to discrimination. Research on wealth inequalities has shown that
low-income minority adolescents often predict that distributions of wealth are
based on individual factors when, in fact, structural obstacles exist
(Arsenio & Willems, 2017 ). This discrepancy has been hypothesized
to be related to stress and depression. Thus, ERS strategies
could focus on providing support and messages about the realities
associated with societal constraints on mobility ( Bañales et al.,
in, press ).
