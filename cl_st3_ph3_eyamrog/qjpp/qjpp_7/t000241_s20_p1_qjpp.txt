The results reported in Table 1 show that the impact
of tourism on income inequality is consistently negative in all
the income inequality specifications used in this study. This has
been confirmed by the coefficient of tourism in the rule
of law (Law), VAC, PS, and Govef, which have been
found to be negative and statistically significant at the 1
percent level of significance. This shows that an increase in
tourism development leads to a decrease in income inequality in
the countries under study. This finding is consistent with some
of the previous studies, such as Haddad et al. (2013)
for the case of Brazil, Raza and Shah (2017) for
the case of 43 top tourist arrival countries, Fauzel (2019)
for the case of Mauritius, and C. P. Nguyen et
al. (2020) for the case of 97 countries, among others.
The results also show that only one proxy of governance
quality (VAC) helps to reduce inequality. This implies that an
increase in the VAC helps to lower the income distribution
of the residents of the countries under study. The results
also show that the interaction between governance and tourism development
is statistically significant in three of the four specifications used,
namely, VAC, PS, and Govef.
