Interestingly, in this study, adolescents’ reasoning revealed their different interpretations
of parental messages, depending on parents’ social status. This resonates
with recent work from Hong Kong, where Asian minority groups
chose between using different languages (i.e., their native vs. the
host language), depending on whether their interaction partners belonged to
social minority or social majority groups (Gu et al., 2017
). In order to justify their choices, they voiced parental
expectations about cross-group interactions as well as negative societal perceptions
about social minority groups living in Hong Kong.
