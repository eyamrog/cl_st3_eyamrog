Peer influence was stated 15 times in six interviews. Consistent
with previous research, it was found that the privileged class,
through peer influence in their direct and indirect social groups,
learned the act of human welfare. Notably, previous studies discussed
peer influence in line with the theory of social learning
(Van Hoorn et al., 2016 ). In comparison, the present
research established the significance of peer influence in both empathy-altruism
and the theory of planned behavior. The study revealed that
peer influence stimulated the concept of building moral values in
the surrounding areas, collectively giving rise to an innovative social
system prevailing on the principles of moral values. This finding
is consistent with that of Joseph and Kuperminc ( 2021
) and Osei ( 2021 ), indicating that peer influence
plays a vital role observed prosocial behavior among individuals and
groups.
