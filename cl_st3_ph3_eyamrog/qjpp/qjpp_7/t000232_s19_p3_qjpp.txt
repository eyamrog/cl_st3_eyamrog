What occurred must be distinguished from a formal change or
a response to a sudden shock—new legislation or a catastrophic
event such as the destruction of a hospital in an
earthquake, for example—which likely would have been addressed using an
explicit change management plan. The movement of FPs out of
the hospital was gradual (in fact, the process is still
ongoing; a small number of urban FPs hold on to
their privileges, and FPs are still ubiquitous in smaller community
hospitals). No solution for notification followed FPs as they left
because there was no formal leaving.
