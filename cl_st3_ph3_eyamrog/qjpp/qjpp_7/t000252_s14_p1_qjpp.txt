Interviews were conducted with 31 (28 men, three women) participants
who resided in the province of Punjab, Pakistan, an affluent
province that is home to more than 60% of the
total population of Pakistan (Kugelman, 2013 ). Participants were between
30 and 40 years of age and Islamic. Twenty-two were
business owners and CEOs of multinationals or NGOs and all
participants were considered members of the privileged class. That is,
they had high social economic status and occupied the “best”
occupations that were not necessarily earned (Hafeez, 1985 ). The
privileged class also refers to people who are employed at
the highest levels in the country, own businesses, are politicians,
or are wealth heirs (Marshall, 1996 ). Members of the
privileged class often receive special support and compensations. Participants must
also have engaged in prosocial behaviors during the previous 5
years. Following Guetterman ( 2015 ), after 31 interviews, theoretical
saturation was achieved and thus no additional participants were recruited.
