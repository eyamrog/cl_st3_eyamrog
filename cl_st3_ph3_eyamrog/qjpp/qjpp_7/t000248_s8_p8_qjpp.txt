The present study focuses on children of immigrants of Turkish
and Moroccan origin in Flanders, the Dutch-speaking part of Belgium.
We sample minority youth from these groups as they constitute
the most discriminated and devalued groups in Belgian society and
in Belgian schools (Alanya et al., 2017 ; Baysu et
al., 2018 ). It is in this context that we
ask how critical socialization experiences of school discrimination and minority
peer presence enable or disrupt the ERS of dual national
and ethnic identities among Turkish and Moroccan origin Belgian minority
youth.
